Have you seen AI in Criminal Justice Today?

An Arizona court commissioner again discounts an AI-drafted police report, this time from Gilbert; Chile tests AI to fill its education-and-work prison units.

Five verified developments today, led by the courts. At a first appearance in Maricopa County, a judicial officer dropped a felony charge brought by Gilbert police and said the weight of the evidence was limited because the report gave little detail on how generative AI drafted the arrest narrative; AZFamily says it is the second such case before the same officer in a couple of weeks. In Chile, the prison service Gendarmería is testing an AI data system to speed applications to its Centros de Educación y Trabajo. Also: Kenya's Judiciary pilots an in-house AI research tool for judges, a New York bill would treat judges' reliance on AI-fabricated law as misconduct, and China's Supreme People's Court consults on draft rules for AI-assisted trials. Nevada: no new development today. From the archive: 17 verified briefs, including an Arizona appeals ruling on an AI-generated victim video at sentencing and Lakeland's Axon Draft One contract.
