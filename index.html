Daily Briefing · November 21, 2026

The UK justice ministry publishes how its reoffending-risk tools work, including models trained on a 2018 caseload; Colorado gives parole officers an AI note-drafting tool.

Eleven verified developments today, led by supervision and sentencing. The UK Ministry of Justice published a transparency record for the risk scores that feed pre-sentence and parole reports in England and Wales; it says the models were trained on a 30 June 2018 caseload and have no fixed retraining schedule. Colorado's corrections department says parole officers have used an optional AI tool to document more than 800 meetings. A reentry nonprofit's chief executive reports an internal pilot of an AI phone coach and says a parole-hearing version is on hold. Also: a Tulane preprint on New Orleans court dockets, a parole-prediction study, an Idaho courts report, a federal evidence-rule update, and facial recognition moves in London, Nottinghamshire and Hong Kong, plus AI-generated most-wanted images in Canada. Nevada: no new development today; the archive adds a June report that Sparks police plate readers had an ICE hotlist enabled. From the archive: 17 verified briefs.
