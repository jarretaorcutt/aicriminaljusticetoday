Daily Briefing · November 24, 2026

Swiss courts have leaned on risk-scoring tools in more than 100 Federal Supreme Court rulings; a Kansas City study finds AI models match statistical risk tools.

Three verified developments today, all on how risk is scored in sentencing and prison decisions. Swissinfo reports that forensic psychiatrists used algorithmic instruments in more than a hundred Federal Supreme Court rulings since 2000, mostly statistical tools rather than AI. UMKC and UC Davis researchers say large language models matched or beat statistical tools at predicting rearrest, but their explanations were not faithful. TU Wien proposes using the lowest score when equally accurate prison risk models disagree. Nevada: no new development today. Gemini's two leads, the ICO audit of the Met's facial recognition and Harris County's withdrawn Clearview contract, were already published. From the archive: three verified briefs.
