An Arizona court commissioner drops a felony charge at a first appearance and says AI drafting limits the weight of a Gilbert police report

It is the second case in a couple of weeks in which the same Maricopa County judicial officer has questioned a probable cause statement written with Axon Draft One, AZFamily reports.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 22, 2026

A Maricopa County judicial officer, described by AZFamily as a commissioner, dismissed a felony aggravated assault charge at a woman's initial appearance in a Gilbert police case and questioned the weight of the evidence because officers used artificial intelligence to help write the probable cause statement, the station reported on Nov. 19, 2026.

Gilbert police had been called to a home near Pecos and Higley roads, where investigators said a woman with a knife threatened family members. In dismissing the felony charge, the commissioner pointed to the report's own wording that no specific threats regarding the knife or bodily harm were reported. Two misdemeanor charges were allowed to move forward.

The commissioner then addressed the last paragraph of the report, which disclosed that AI was used to write the probable cause statement: "Although probable cause was found, the weight of the evidence is limited, given that there is limited information provided about the extent of the usage and contribution of generative artificial intelligence that was used in drafting the arrest narrative allegations."

AZFamily reports it is the second such case in a couple of weeks involving the same judicial officer. In the earlier case, which involved the Arizona Department of Public Safety, the officer let a DUI suspect go free, citing the same reason.

Gilbert police told the station they use Axon Draft One and have used it for more than a year. "Officers are required to review and verify that all information in the report is accurate," the department said, adding that narratives are based on body-worn camera recordings and officers' input.

Katie Kinsey of the Policing Project at NYU Law School told AZFamily that a probable cause statement justifies an arrest, a search or another enforcement action and is "a really high-risk document." Jonathan Wroblewski, a senior fellow with the Council on Criminal Justice, said officer review is one step toward reliability but that other steps are probably needed. The judicial officer and the defendant were not named in the report.

What we know

  • A Maricopa County judicial officer dismissed a felony aggravated assault charge at an initial appearance in a Gilbert police case; two misdemeanor charges went forward (AZFamily).
  • The officer said the weight of the evidence is limited because the report gave limited information about how generative AI was used to draft the arrest narrative (AZFamily).
  • It is the second such case in a couple of weeks before the same officer; the earlier one involved the Arizona Department of Public Safety (AZFamily).
  • Gilbert police say they use Axon Draft One, have used it for more than a year, and require officers to review and verify reports (AZFamily).

What remains unknown

  • Whether prosecutors will refile or challenge the rulings, and whether other Maricopa County judicial officers take the same approach.
  • What the full disclosure paragraph in the Gilbert report says; AZFamily did not publish the report.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Disclosure paragraphs let judges see when AI helped draft a report and weigh it accordingly.

Risks and limitations

  • Charging decisions and pretrial liberty can turn on wording produced or shaped by a drafting tool.
  • Different judicial officers may treat AI-assisted reports differently, producing uneven outcomes.
