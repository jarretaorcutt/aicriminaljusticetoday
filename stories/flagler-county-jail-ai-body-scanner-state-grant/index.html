A Florida sheriff's office will replace its jail body scanner with one that uses AI to help detect contraband, paid for by a state grant

The Flagler County Sheriff's Office says the scanner is part of $2.4 million in state grant funding announced Nov. 16, 2026. The release names no vendor and gives no detail on how the AI works.

The Flagler County Sheriff's Office in Florida says its jail is getting a new whole-body security scanner that uses "not only" modern x-ray technology "but also artificial intelligence to help detect contraband," according to an agency news release dated Monday, November 16, 2026.

The release says the office was awarded $2.4 million in state grant funding through the Florida Department of Law Enforcement. Nearly $1.5 million of it is allotted to replace a 20-year-old computer-aided dispatch system. Other items include handheld drug detection devices and a Rapid DNA system.

At the Sheriff Perry Hall Inmate Detention Facility, the new scanner replaces the current 10-year-old scanner, which the office says has reached the end of its service life. The grant also pays for an X-ray conveyor scanner to screen bags and property entering the jail, walk-through metal detectors, smoke inhalation masks and a digital video workstation.

The agency says the award is in addition to a state grant it announced in July, bringing its total to more than $8.1 million, and that because the costs are covered by the state, county taxpayers will not directly pay for the equipment.

The release does not name the scanner's maker, describe what the artificial intelligence does or how accurate it is, or say when the scanner will be installed.

  • It replaces a 10-year-old scanner at the Sheriff Perry Hall Inmate Detention Facility (agency release).
