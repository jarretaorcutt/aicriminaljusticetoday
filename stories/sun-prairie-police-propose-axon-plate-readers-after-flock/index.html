Sun Prairie, Wisconsin, police propose moving plate readers from Flock to Axon as residents object

From the archive (Oct 16, 2026): The chief offered a 14-day data retention trial; opponents said a new vendor doesn't answer their concerns.

Sun Prairie, Wisconsin, announced on Sept. 16 that it would not renew its Flock contract, and at an Oct. 7 Public Safety Committee meeting Police Chief Kevin Warych proposed switching the plate-reader service to Axon, The Star (HNGnews) reported. The department has six cameras that expire on Dec. 31, 2026, and three that expire on March 31, 2027. Warych proposed cutting the retention window for plate captures to a 14-day trial period and promised the department will never share the data with federal authorities, according to the report. Eight people registered against the Axon contract at the meeting and 20 residents submitted forms in opposition.

What we know

  • The city announced on Sept. 16 that it would not renew its Flock contract (The Star/HNGnews).
  • The chief proposed an Axon contract and a 14-day retention trial at the Oct. 7 committee meeting (The Star/HNGnews).
  • Six cameras expire on Dec. 31, 2026, and three on March 31, 2027 (The Star/HNGnews).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the city approved an Axon contract; the report describes only the proposal.
  • What a data-sharing agreement with Axon would say.
