UK Home Affairs Committee opens an inquiry into technology in policing, including live facial recognition and AI

Written evidence is invited until Wednesday 3 February.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 26, 2026

The Home Affairs Committee announced a new inquiry, Technology in Policing, on 25 November 2026. The committee says the Government's Police Reform White Paper raised concerns about patchy adoption of new technologies across forces and proposed centralised national procurement under a new National Police Service. The announcement lists Live Facial Recognition, robotic process automation that enters crime-report data into police databases, and AI used to triage non-emergency calls and for note taking and transcription. It notes that attempts to modernise the Police National Database were abandoned after failing to meet "time, cost and quality standards", and that concerns have been raised about potential biases in facial recognition. Committee Chair Dame Karen Bradley said the committee wants to know what safeguards are in place to ensure data is handled safely and used for acceptable purposes. The inquiry asks five questions, including how forces are adopting AI, what barriers exist, whether the white paper proposals will help, what safeguards protect data security and what governance keeps use proportionate and maintains public confidence. The deadline for written submissions is Wednesday 3 February.

What we know

  • The inquiry was announced on 25 November 2026 (UK Parliament committee page).
  • It covers Live Facial Recognition, robotic process automation and AI for call triage and back-office work (UK Parliament committee page).
  • Written submissions close Wednesday 3 February (UK Parliament committee page).

What remains unknown

  • Who will give oral evidence and when hearings will be held.
  • What the committee will recommend.

Risks and limitations

  • The committee lists risks from increased use of AI and concerns about bias in facial recognition.
