Wausau, Wisconsin, committee schedules closed-door legal advice on ending its $250,000 Flock contract

From the archive (Oct 18, 2026): The city pays $50,000 a year for 20 cameras; officials disagree on whether a contract clause lets it stop paying.

Wausau's Public Health and Safety Committee scheduled a closed session to confer with counsel on litigation the city is or is likely to become involved in "regarding termination of contract with Flock Group Inc.," the Wausau Pilot & Review reported on Oct. 18, 2026. The city pays Flock $50,000 a year for 20 cameras under a five-year, $250,000 agreement that runs through Dec. 15, 2028. Section 11.16 of the contract allows termination for non-appropriation, but Police Chief Matthew Barnes reads it as covering the city's ability to pay rather than a choice to stop paying, according to the report. Mayor Doug Diny's 2027 budget proposal includes the payment. The outlet reports that more than a dozen Wisconsin agencies have ended or suspended Flock programs this year.

What we know

  • The contract is a five-year, $250,000 agreement for 20 cameras that runs through Dec. 15, 2028 (Wausau Pilot & Review).
  • The committee's agenda listed a closed session on litigation regarding termination of the contract (Wausau Pilot & Review).
  • The mayor's 2027 budget proposal includes the $50,000 payment (Wausau Pilot & Review).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Flock has threatened legal action; the agenda does not say.
  • What the committee decided after the closed session.
