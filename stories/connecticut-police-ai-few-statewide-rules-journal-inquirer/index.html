Connecticut has no statewide inventory or general rules for police AI, beyond a prosecutors' pause on AI-written reports

Journal Inquirer reporting distributed by TNS says local departments are left to set their own policies.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 26, 2026

Journal Inquirer reporter Jordan Nathaniel Fenster, in a story distributed by TNS and published by Governing on Nov. 25, 2026, reports that beyond automated license plate readers, Connecticut has no general statewide inventory showing which AI systems local police use, how they use them or what safeguards apply. The only statewide guidance on police AI is a moratorium on AI programs that draft, author or narrate criminal reports, which Chief State's Attorney Patrick Griffin imposed in April. The state's Responsible AI Framework, created in 2023, applies to state agencies and their contractors, while local governments are only encouraged to adopt it. In New Haven, a 2024 contract with Axon included Draft One, which can use body-camera audio to generate a draft police report; the story says the contract shows the department could use the software, not how widely officers did, and a department spokesman said the trial was not department-wide and that the department does not currently have a policy on AI use. The Office of the Chief Public Defender proposed a bill in January that would have required AI-generated reports to identify the software used, carry an officer's signature and keep audit logs; no AI report-writing proposals moved forward in the 2026 legislative session. The story notes there are no reports of AI report writing being used in Connecticut.

What we know

  • Beyond license plate readers there is no general statewide inventory of police AI (Journal Inquirer via TNS).
  • The only statewide guidance is a prosecutors' moratorium on AI-drafted criminal reports imposed in April (Journal Inquirer via TNS).
  • No AI report-writing proposals moved forward in the 2026 legislative session (Journal Inquirer via TNS).

What remains unknown

  • Which Connecticut departments use AI tools beyond report writing.
  • Whether the legislature will take up the public defender's proposal.

Risks and limitations

  • Without inventories or local policies, the public cannot see which AI tools police use or what safeguards apply.
