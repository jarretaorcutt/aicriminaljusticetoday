Little Rock weighs an ordinance adding oversight rules for police surveillance technology after a plate reader misuse audit

The proposal covers license plate readers, facial recognition, gunshot detection, drones and cameras with automated analytical capabilities; a third and final reading is set for Dec. 1.

Little Rock city directors are considering an ordinance that would establish new transparency, reporting and oversight requirements for the Little Rock Police Department's use of surveillance technology, KATV reported on Nov. 18, 2026. The proposal, sponsored by At-Large Director Antwan Phillips, received its second reading at the board's Nov. 17 meeting and is scheduled for a third and final reading on Dec. 1. If approved, the rules would take effect Jan. 1, 2027.

The ordinance covers automatic license plate readers, facial recognition systems, acoustic gunshot detection systems, drones, cell-site simulators, social media monitoring tools and cameras with automated analytical capabilities, KATV reported. Body-worn and dashboard cameras are excluded.

The proposal follows an October audit of the department's license plate reader system that found three officers had misused it, including one who ran 28 searches of the same plate over a 10-day period, KATV reported. The officers were relieved of duty, Mayor Frank Scott Jr. confirmed.

What remains unknown

  • Whether the board will pass the ordinance on Dec. 1, and in what form.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Public reporting and audits could catch misuse of surveillance tools sooner.

Risks and limitations

  • Reporting rules do not by themselves limit which tools police may use.
