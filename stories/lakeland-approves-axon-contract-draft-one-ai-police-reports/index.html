Lakeland, Florida, approves a nearly $22 million Axon contract that includes Draft One AI report writing

From the archive (Oct 9, 2026): Florida does not require police to disclose which reports were drafted with AI, WMNF notes.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 22, 2026
Story type

Jurisdiction

AI application

Lakeland's city commission approved a new contract with Axon worth nearly $22 million that includes Draft One, an AI tool that listens to conversations through body cameras and uses the dialogue to generate a report, WMNF reported on October 9, 2026. The tool is already used by the Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office. WMNF notes that in Florida, police aren't required to disclose which reports are generated with the use of AI, and that the original draft isn't stored. It also recounts a Utah case in which Draft One wrote that an officer transformed into a frog after the body camera picked up a movie playing in the background.

What we know

  • Lakeland approved a nearly $22 million Axon contract that includes Draft One (WMNF, October 9, 2026).
  • Florida does not require disclosure of AI-drafted reports, and the original draft isn't stored (WMNF).

What remains unknown

  • When Lakeland officers began using Draft One and for which report types.
