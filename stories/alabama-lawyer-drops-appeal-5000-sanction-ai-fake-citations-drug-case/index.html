An Alabama defense lawyer drops his appeal of a $5,000 sanction for AI-generated fake citations in a drug conspiracy case

A federal appeals court dismissed the appeal after the lawyer said the 12 months of required disclosures had already run, FOX10 News reports.

A Spanish Fort, Alabama, lawyer has dropped his appeal of sanctions that a federal judge imposed on him for using artificial intelligence to file a faulty motion in a criminal drug conspiracy case, FOX10 News reported on Nov. 20, 2026.

According to FOX10, the motion asked U.S. District Judge Terry Moorer to postpone the trial and contained several citations to nonexistent court cases. Moorer fined the lawyer $5,000 and ordered him to disclose the sanctions to all of his clients for the next 12 months.

The lawyer told the Atlanta-based appeals court there was no reason to continue now that the 12 months have passed, and the court agreed Thursday to dismiss the appeal, FOX10 reports. In his filing he wrote that he still disputes the "factual and legal basis for the sanctions" and that dropping the appeal should not be read as accepting the punishment; he said he has already paid the fine.

The defendant in the underlying case has since been convicted, FOX10 reports.
