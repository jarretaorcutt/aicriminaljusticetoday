San Diego City Council rescinds police use policies for license plate readers and Smart Streetlights; the mayor says he will not veto

The council kept 52 other surveillance use policies in its annual TRUST Ordinance review. A payment of about 2 million dollars tied to the contract was due in December.

The San Diego City Council voted on Monday, November 16 to rescind the San Diego Police Department's use policy for automated license plate readers, the San Diego Chronicle reported. The motion passed unanimously among the members present, with Councilmembers Campbell and von Wilpert absent, according to the Chronicle's account of the meeting recording.

The vote came under Item 200, the annual review of police surveillance technologies required by the city's Transparent and Responsible Use of Surveillance Technology Ordinance, known as the TRUST Ordinance. The police department had asked the council to approve continued use of 54 surveillance technologies, the Chronicle reported.

The council approved the remaining 52 surveillance use policies 7 to 0, and rescinded the Smart Streetlights surveillance use policy by 5 to 2, according to the Chronicle. Budget staff told the council that a payment of about 2 million dollars connected to the contract was due in mid to late December, and that acting before it went out would produce that saving in fiscal year 2027, the Chronicle reported.

Mayor Todd Gloria said in a statement: "I respect the Council's decision and will not veto either action." He said the tools had given officers a way to investigate serious crimes, but that "public trust is essential for their continued use." In a statement on Friday, November 13, he had said he would not use his veto if the council voted to end the program.

The vote follows an October council committee vote to end the city's use of the plate readers, which we reported at the time.
