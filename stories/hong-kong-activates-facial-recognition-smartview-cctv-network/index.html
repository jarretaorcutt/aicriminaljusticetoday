Hong Kong begins switching on facial recognition across its police CCTV network, with 60,000 camera points planned by 2028

Police say access needs prior authorisation and is logged; the system's stated purposes include national security.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
Hong Kong
AI application
facial recognition, video analytics
AI framework
Not yet labeled

Hong Kong has begun activating facial recognition on SmartView, the police-run CCTV network, the Macau-based outlet Plataforma reported on Nov. 16, citing Police Commissioner Joe Chow. The report says the expansion is budgeted at four billion local dollars and aims to reach 60,000 capture points across the city by 2028.

According to Plataforma, the network currently includes around five thousand state devices and six thousand external feeds, and a second phase will add 20,000 terminals. Chow said the identification tools will primarily serve to locate missing persons and solve serious crimes and that strict oversight protocols apply. Secretary for Security Chris Tang said negotiations with operators of private commercial complexes to connect their cameras were in the final stages, without naming them.

The Hong Kong Police Force's SmartView page, which we also checked, lists the system's purposes, beginning with safeguarding national security and the prevention, detection and investigation of crimes. It says the facial recognition function "does not involve automatic and large-scale identification of members of the public," that unsuccessful matches are deleted automatically and immediately, and that footage and metadata are not retained for more than 31 days and 180 days respectively. Access requires eligibility, prior authorisation and verification, and each access leaves a logged audit trail, the page says.

Those approvals are internal to the police; the page does not describe a court warrant. Plataforma, drawing on the news agency EFE, notes fears among rights groups of convergence with mainland China's surveillance model. The groups are not named in the report.

What we know

  • Facial recognition is being activated on the SmartView network, which is planned to reach 60,000 capture points by 2028 (Plataforma, Nov. 16, 2026).
  • Police say footage is kept up to 31 days and metadata up to 180 days (Hong Kong Police Force SmartView page).
  • Stated purposes include national security and crime investigation (Hong Kong Police Force SmartView page).

What remains unknown

  • Which private operators will connect their cameras.
  • How many searches are run and how many are refused authorisation.
  • Whether any body outside the police reviews individual searches.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Published retention limits and logged access give a baseline that can be checked.
  • Police cite locating missing people as a main use.

Risks and limitations

  • Authorisation sits inside the police force.
  • National security is a listed purpose in a city where that law has been used widely against dissent, as the report notes.
