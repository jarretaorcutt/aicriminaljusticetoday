Harris County pulls a proposed Clearview AI facial recognition contract from its commissioners' agenda

The sheriff's office said the agreement was not ready for consideration and would not say whether it will return.

Harris County, Texas, commissioners have put off consideration of a facial recognition contract with Clearview AI, ABC13 Houston reported on Nov. 19, 2026. The Harris County Sheriff's Office said the item was removed from the Thursday, Nov. 19 agenda because it was not ready for consideration, and it would not say whether it would return for consideration at a later date.

According to the commissioners' agenda before the item was removed, the proposed agreement was intended to help reduce violent crime countywide and assist investigators with cases that have no leads, ABC13 reports. Clearview AI says its database contains more than 100 billion facial images collected from public web sources, including social media and mugshot websites.

"A match is not a proof. It's more like a good guess," Dorothy Jiang, a University of Houston professor and co-director of its Human-Centered AI Institute, told ABC13. The station pointed to a lawsuit in which a Tennessee woman says she was wrongfully identified in a North Dakota police investigation that used Clearview AI and spent six months in jail. Clearview AI says search results are intended to serve as investigative leads, not definitive identifications.

Risks and limitations
