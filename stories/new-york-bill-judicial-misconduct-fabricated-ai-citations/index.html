New York bill would make it judicial misconduct to put fabricated legal authority in a ruling

State Sen. Michelle Hinchey's bill says using AI alone is not misconduct, but knowingly or recklessly including made-up citations would be, Reuters reports.

New York State Sen. Michelle Hinchey, a Democrat, introduced the "judicial artificial intelligence accountability and integrity act" on Monday, Reuters reported on Nov. 17. Under the bill, a state judge or justice who knowingly or recklessly includes fabricated legal authority in a ruling would be deemed to have engaged in misconduct.

The New York State Commission on Judicial Conduct could then issue sanctions or send its misconduct finding to state lawmakers to consider impeachment, according to the report. The use of AI alone would not constitute misconduct. Reuters said the commission did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Reuters notes that two federal judges, in Mississippi and New Jersey, acknowledged after an inquiry by Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley that staff had used AI to help prepare court orders, and that an Oklahoma state judge acknowledged a ruling contained fictitious AI-generated citations. New York's court system already has an administrative policy on AI use by judges and court staff.

What we know

  • The bill would deem a judge who knowingly or recklessly includes fabricated legal authority to have engaged in misconduct (Reuters via KFGO).
  • The Commission on Judicial Conduct could sanction or refer the finding to lawmakers to consider impeachment (Reuters).
  • Use of AI alone would not be misconduct under the bill (Reuters).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the bill has co-sponsors or a committee hearing scheduled.
  • How "recklessly" would be applied to a judge's staff drafting with AI.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Sets a clear rule for judges whose rulings decide liberty in criminal cases.

Risks and limitations

  • Misconduct findings depend on proving a judge's knowledge or recklessness, which may be hard to show.
