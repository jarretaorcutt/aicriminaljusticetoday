A Texas federal judge denies a defendant's release before sentencing and says he will sanction defense lawyers over AI-generated motions

Judge Mark Pittman called the filings "blatant AI slop" in a voting-related criminal case, Business Insider reports.

In an order denying a defendant's request to be released from custody ahead of sentencing in a voting-related criminal case, U.S. District Judge Mark Pittman wrote that the defense's arguments were "mostly generated by artificial intelligence" and legally flawed, Business Insider reported.

The defendant, a 70-year-old lawful permanent resident according to court papers, was convicted of a felony count of false statements of citizenship in order to register to vote and a misdemeanor count. His three lawyers filed a motion, which disclosed that portions were prepared "with the assistance of a generative artificial intelligence tool," arguing that because sentencing guidelines recommend zero to six months in prison, he should be exempt from pre-sentence detention.

"Defendant is not a pro se litigant; he has three counsel of record with decades of practice between them," Pittman wrote. He said the filings raise serious questions about the lawyers' ability to practice law and that he would sanction them, but lacked time on his docket to schedule a hearing before Thanksgiving. "The Court is exhausted by these antics," he wrote, warning that further waste of resources could bring sanctions including a ban on practicing in the Northern District of Texas.

The lead defense attorney responded to Business Insider by email. We do not name the defendant or the lawyers, who have not yet been sanctioned.

What we know

  • Judge Mark Pittman denied release pending sentencing and said the arguments were mostly AI-generated (Business Insider).
  • The defense motion disclosed that portions were prepared with a generative AI tool (Business Insider).
  • Pittman said he would sanction the three defense lawyers but had no time for a hearing before Thanksgiving (Business Insider).

What remains unknown

  • What sanctions the court will impose and when the hearing will be held.
  • Whether the defendant's sentencing is affected by the dispute.

Risks and limitations

  • A person's custody before sentencing can turn on filings that a judge finds legally empty.
  • Defendants may not know their lawyers relied on AI until a judge says so.
