Madhya Pradesh High Court says the state government cited an AI-generated, non-existent Supreme Court ruling

Justice Vivek Kumar Singh said the fabricated citation "borders on actively misleading the Court" and told the Advocate General's office to verify citations, The Indian Express reports.

The Madhya Pradesh High Court has rebuked the state government for citing an AI-generated, non-existent Supreme Court judgment in its legal submissions, The Indian Express reported on Nov 20, 2026.

According to the newspaper, Justice Vivek Kumar Singh said the state cited a ruling titled 'State of Andhra Pradesh vs S.S. Mohammad Nisar' with the citation (2008) 5 SCC 230. The court found that the citation actually belongs to a different case about suspending a sentence in a criminal appeal. "It is glaringly apparent that the respondents have blindly resorted to the use of Artificial Intelligence tools to draft their pleadings," the court said, as quoted by The Indian Express.

The court said inserting fictitious judgments without verifying them "wastes precious judicial time but also borders on actively misleading the Court", cautioned the state against "reckless practices", and said the office of the Advocate General was expected to make sure drafting counsel verify every citation before it is placed on record.

The remarks came in a petition by a former police officer who was dismissed from service in May 2024 without an inquiry. The court set aside the dismissal and ordered his reinstatement, while allowing the state to hold a regular departmental enquiry, the newspaper reported.

What we know

  • The state's submissions cited a non-existent Supreme Court ruling with the citation (2008) 5 SCC 230, which belongs to a different case (The Indian Express).
  • Justice Vivek Kumar Singh said the state had blindly resorted to AI tools and warned against "reckless practices" (The Indian Express).
  • The court set aside the officer's dismissal and ordered reinstatement (The Indian Express).

What remains unknown

  • Which AI tool was used and who in the state's legal team drafted the pleading.
  • Whether the court imposed any sanction beyond its warning.

Risks and limitations

  • A government litigant relying on unverified AI output can mislead a court in cases that decide people's jobs and liberty.
