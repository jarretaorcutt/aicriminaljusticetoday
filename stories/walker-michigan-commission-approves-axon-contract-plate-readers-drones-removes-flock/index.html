Walker, Michigan approves a $3.64 million Axon contract with plate readers and drones, and will remove Flock

Residents objected to more cameras; the commission approved the deal unanimously on its consent agenda.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 24, 2026

mlive.com reports that at its last meeting before new members are sworn in, the Walker City Commission approved a $3.64 million contract for automatic license plate reader cameras, drones and data storage. The agreement extends the city's existing contract with Axon through Sept. 30, 2034 and, from April 2027, adds a records management system. According to public safety officials, it includes 10 automatic license plate readers and two drones, and city officials said Walker would remove its existing Flock license plate readers.

The commission approved the contract unanimously as part of its consent agenda at the Monday, Nov. 23 meeting. A group of residents opposed an increase in the number of plate readers, with several citing Fourth Amendment privacy concerns and the potential for misuse, and some urging the commission to wait for incoming members. Police Chief Brandyn Heugel said the agreement would let the city add an updated records management system.
