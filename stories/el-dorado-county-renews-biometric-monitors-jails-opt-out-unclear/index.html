El Dorado County, California, renews biometric monitors for people in its jails as questions remain about whether they can refuse them

Supervisors voted 5-0 on Nov. 17 to keep the 4Sight Labs devices, which track location and vital signs, the Sacramento Bee reports. The sheriff's public policy manual does not address the system.

El Dorado County, California, will keep using wearable biometric devices on people held in its jails, the Sacramento Bee reported on Nov. 19, 2026. The Board of Supervisors approved the renewal in a 5-0 vote as part of its consent calendar, which lets supervisors approve multiple items without separate discussion.

According to the Bee, the devices track a person's location, heart rate, blood oxygen levels and skin temperature and send the information to jail staff to help detect medical emergencies. The system, called Overwatch, is designed by Arizona-based 4Sight Labs and has been in use at the county's South Lake Tahoe and Placerville jail facilities since the spring. The Bee describes the devices as locking wristbands; the county's agenda item calls them "wearable biometric health ankle monitors."

The county record says the devices continuously collect physiological data and transmit it through software that "identifies potential changes in an inmate's health condition requiring medical attention." It authorizes a purchase order with 4Sight Labs in the estimated amount of $108,000 for a retroactive term of October 9, 2026, to October 9, 2027, paid from the Inmate Trust Fund.

The Bee reports that the county will purchase 50 subscriptions this year instead of 100, cutting the annual cost roughly in half, to $107,000, and that the renewal includes an agreement under HIPAA governing how outside companies handle protected health information.

What remains unclear, according to the Bee, is how the Sheriff's Office decides who must wear the trackers and whether people can decline. The office's publicly available policy manual does not address the technology, and officials did not respond to the paper's questions. The Bee notes the program is voluntary in San Diego County.

What we know

  • El Dorado County supervisors approved the renewal in a 5-0 vote on the consent calendar (Sacramento Bee, Nov. 19, 2026).
  • The devices track location, heart rate, blood oxygen levels and skin temperature (Sacramento Bee).
  • The county authorized a purchase order with 4Sight Labs in the estimated amount of $108,000, funded by the Inmate Trust Fund (El Dorado County agenda item 26-1572).
  • The county will purchase 50 subscriptions instead of 100 (Sacramento Bee).

What remains unknown

  • Whether people held in El Dorado County jails can decline to wear the devices.
  • How the Sheriff's Office decides who must wear one; its public policy manual does not address the system.
  • How long the health and location data is kept and who can see it.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The Sheriff's Office says the system can trigger a faster medical response, especially overnight or during periods of low staffing (Sacramento Bee).

Risks and limitations

  • Continuous health and location tracking is running without a published policy on consent or use, the Bee's reporting indicates.
