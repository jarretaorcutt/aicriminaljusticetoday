Sacramento approves a $17.7 million Axon master agreement bundling police surveillance and camera contracts through 2031

The council voted 6-1 despite public opposition; terms on data ownership and contract exit still need Axon's agreement, CapRadio reports.

Sacramento's City Council approved a master agreement with Axon on Tuesday night that bundles $17.7 million worth of surveillance technology contracts through 2031, CapRadio reported on Nov. 18, 2026. The vote was 6-1, with Councilmember Mai Vang voting no.

The agreement consolidates six contracts. It renews the FUSUS real-time video and data platform and interview room recording software, and includes body camera devices for police and fire, police car camera systems and a tracking platform for illegal drones, according to CapRadio.

Councilmember Lisa Kaplan proposed clauses stating the city is the sole owner of its data, a 30-day contract termination clause and retention limits for license plate data. If Axon rejects any of the terms, the contract will come back to the council. Mayor Kevin McCarty supported the clauses and said plate-reader technology helps solve crimes.

Over 20 public commenters spoke, many against the deal; advocates have raised concerns for over a year that federal immigration enforcement could access the data. "Surveillance should never become a substitute for public safety," Vang said. The city has used Axon as an alternative to contracting with Flock.

What we know

  • The council approved the $17.7 million master agreement 6-1 (CapRadio).
  • It consolidates six contracts through 2031, including FUSUS, body cameras and car cameras (CapRadio).
  • Proposed clauses cover data ownership, a 30-day termination clause and plate data retention limits (CapRadio).
  • If Axon rejects any term, the contract returns to the council (CapRadio).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Axon accepts the added terms.
  • What retention limit will apply to license plate data.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The added clauses would give the city ownership of its data and a way to exit within 30 days (CapRadio).

Risks and limitations

  • Bundling contracts may make it harder to opt out of single services, critics told the council (CapRadio).
