New York bill would bar the prison and parole department from using AI in parole release and supervision decisions

From the archive (May 14, 2026): Senate bill S10349, still in committee, names COMPAS as the system it targets.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 22, 2026

S10349 would amend New York's executive law and correction law so the Department of Corrections and Community Supervision may not use artificial intelligence in evaluating risk and needs for parole, in deciding who is released to parole supervision, in setting the level of supervision, or in developing transitional accountability plans, the Senate's bill page shows. Each release, supervision level and plan would have to be approved in writing by the parole board, the commissioner or a designee.

The sponsor memo says the COMPAS program, which it calls 'an early artificial intelligence application,' has been used by DOCCS for over a decade and has never undergone peer review or algorithmic testing for biases. The bill was referred to the Crime Victims, Crime And Correction committee on May 14, 2026; an Assembly version, A11320, is also in committee. It has not passed either house.

What we know

  • S10349 was introduced by Sen. Shelley B. Mayer and referred to committee on May 14, 2026 (NY State Senate).
  • It would bar DOCCS from using AI in parole release, supervision-level and transitional accountability plan decisions (NY State Senate).
  • The sponsor memo says COMPAS has been used by DOCCS for over a decade (NY State Senate).

What remains unknown

  • Whether either house will take the bill up, and whether COMPAS meets the state law definitions of AI the bill uses.
