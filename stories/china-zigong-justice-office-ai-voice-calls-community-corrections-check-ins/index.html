A justice office in Zigong, China, uses AI voice calls to check where people on community correction are

Zigong Media says the Gaofeng office's automated calls have run more than 120 spot checks, with staff following up on any answer that doesn't match the records.

The Gaofeng judicial office in Zigong has put artificial intelligence to work on a daily task: checking that people on community correction are where they are supposed to be, Zigong Media reported on Nov. 23, 2026. Community correction is China's system of supervising people in the community instead of in custody.

According to the report, the office randomly selects people from its list and the system places an automated call. Once connected, it verifies identity, asks for the person's location and compares the answers with the information on file. Answers that don't fit, locations that don't match, or refusals to answer are reported to staff, who follow up and visit in person.

Zigong Media reports that the AI voice checks have run more than 120 spot checks so far, and that no one under community correction has slipped out of supervision. The office also says it has completed an individual risk assessment for 100% of the people it supervises, scoring each one on factors such as their offense, family relationships, work skills and behavior; the report does not say that this scoring uses AI. The office also held 2 mental health lectures this year.

The report does not name the vendor, the cost or how accurate the voice system is.

What we know

  • The office randomly selects people and an automated AI voice system calls them to verify identity and location (Zigong Media).
  • Mismatched answers, wrong locations or refusals are passed to staff for follow-up and in-person visits (Zigong Media).
  • The AI voice checks have run more than 120 spot checks so far (Zigong Media).
  • The office reports individual risk assessments for 100% of people it supervises (Zigong Media).

What remains unknown

  • Which company built the voice system and what it costs.
  • How often the system misidentifies someone or misreads an answer, and what happens to a person flagged in error.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Automated calls let a small office check on people more often without more staff (Zigong Media).

Risks and limitations

  • People can be flagged for in-person follow-up because of a bad connection or a misheard answer.
