Spain's data regulator tells two towns to add safeguards before AI video surveillance

From the archive (Oct 9, 2026): One project has facial recognition features; the other adds 38 cameras with AI person search.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 20, 2026
Story type
Archive
Jurisdiction
Spain
AI application
AI video analytics, facial recognition
AI framework
Not yet labeled

Spain's Data Protection Agency (AEPD) warned two municipalities to put the necessary data protection safeguards in place before proceeding with AI-powered video surveillance projects, Biometric Update reported on Oct. 9; the AEPD issued the warning in a press release on October 6.

In one project, footage would be viewed centrally from local police facilities, and the documentation did not establish whether its facial detection and recognition functions would be activated. The second would integrate 38 new cameras into a system covering 124 existing cameras and let staff search recordings for a person matching selected attributes, according to the report.

What we know

  • The AEPD warned two municipalities in an October 6 press release (Biometric Update).
  • One project has facial detection and recognition functions (Biometric Update).
  • The other adds 38 cameras to 124 existing ones with AI person search (Biometric Update).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the facial recognition functions will be switched on.
