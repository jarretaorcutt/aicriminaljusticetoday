Vallejo oversight panels question whether audits of about 5,000 plate-reader searches a quarter are big enough

From the archive (Oct 16, 2026): Police check 30 random Flock searches at a time. Commissioners wanted a larger sample; the department cited staffing.

Vallejo's Police Oversight and Accountability Commission and Surveillance Advisory Board met jointly to review the police department's Flock Safety license-plate reader policies and audit plan, the Vallejo Sun reported on Oct. 16. The bodies have not yet made recommendations to the City Council on whether to renew the contract.

The audit plan pulls three months of data and checks 30 random individual searches at a time. Commission chair John Lewis questioned whether that sample was large enough, given about 5,000 Flock searches each quarter; police staff said the method reflected staffing levels. The department reported 99 standard plate-reader cameras, 213 gunshot detection devices and 163 people who conduct searches, with data stored for 30 days. It said 293 agencies share data with Vallejo, 271 in-state and 22 outside the state, and its report noted there is no legal requirement to obtain a search warrant before accessing Flock data.
