Police Scotland is trialling BriefCam AI search on around 100 Aberdeen CCTV cameras, without facial recognition

Operators search footage by descriptions like a 'red jacket' or a 'white car'; the three-month pilot runs until 7 December 2026 and could be rolled out nationally.

Police Scotland is trialling BriefCam, an AI-powered CCTV search system, on around 100 cameras in and around Aberdeen city centre, the force says. The three-month pilot started on Monday, 7 September and runs until Monday, 7 December 2026, and will assess whether the system is suitable for a national roll out.

Operators search footage using descriptions such as a 'red jacket' or a 'white car.' In one case, Police Scotland says, nine hours of footage was analysed in around one hour to trace an elderly woman, narrowing over 8,000 objects down to six possible options.

The BBC reported on 20 November that the cameras can run facial recognition but this trial does not use it, and Assistant Chief Constable Tim Mairs said the force had "no intention" to switch that capability on. The BBC said a public consultation earlier in the year received "mixed reviews." Mairs said the tool is used by experienced CCTV operators and does not replace staff.

What we know

  • The pilot uses around 100 cameras and runs from 7 September to 7 December 2026 (Police Scotland).
  • In one case, nine hours of footage was analysed in around one hour, narrowing over 8,000 objects to six (Police Scotland).
  • The trial does not use facial recognition (BBC News).

What remains unknown

  • What the pilot evaluation will find and whether it will be published.
  • How long searched footage and results are kept.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Police Scotland says it can cut the time to find missing vulnerable people and locate suspects (Police Scotland).

Risks and limitations

  • The cameras can run facial recognition; any future use would need to go through a similar trial process (BBC News).
