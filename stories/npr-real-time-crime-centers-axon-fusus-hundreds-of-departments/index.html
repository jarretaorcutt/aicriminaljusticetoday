NPR reports hundreds of police departments run real-time crime centers, with Axon's Fusus pulling in body camera, dashcam and 911 data

From the archive (Oct 8, 2026): Privacy experts say removing friction makes investigations easier to expand, while officers say the tools save time.

In an NPR report republished by KSMU on Oct. 8, 2026, correspondent Meg Anderson wrote that hundreds of police departments have a central real-time crime center hub. Axon describes Fusus as a rapidly deployable real-time crime center in the cloud, and the report says its data sources include police body cameras, dashcams and 911 dispatch. Fusus use was just over 100 law enforcement agencies in 2024, and Axon says the number has more than doubled. Law professor Andrew Guthrie Ferguson and the Brennan Center's Rachel Levinson-Waldman raised concerns about broad surveillance. Axon did not respond to requests for comment.

What we know

  • Hundreds of police departments have real-time crime centers (NPR via KSMU).
  • Fusus agency use was just over 100 in 2024, and Axon says it has more than doubled (NPR via KSMU).
  • Axon did not respond to requests for comment (NPR via KSMU).

What remains unknown

  • How widely AI is used across crime centers; the report does not specify.
  • How courts will treat the surveillance; the report says they are moving slowly.
