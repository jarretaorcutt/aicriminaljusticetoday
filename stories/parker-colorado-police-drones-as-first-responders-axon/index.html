Parker, Colorado, adds Drones as First Responders through a renewed Axon agreement, aiming for 90-second arrivals

The town council approved a $516,811 agreement for 2027 that also adds AI body-camera tools and license plate readers.

The Douglas County News-Press reports that on Nov. 16 the Parker town council approved a renewed master agreement with Axon in the amount of $516,811 for 2027 to add Drones as First Responders. The department plans three drones stationed in three docks, plus three patrol-led drones. "The goal is from the time the call comes in to (when) the drone is on scene within 90 seconds," Commander Jake Schuster said.

The agreement also includes AI-enabled body-camera tools for translation and policy review, automated license plate readers, and dispatch tools that will likely involve an AI system answering calls on non-emergency lines, the paper reports. Schuster said plate-reader data is deleted from the department's system after seven days. Deputy Chief Chris Peters said the department intends to publish a transparency page showing previous flights and their purpose.

What remains unknown

Benefits and opportunities

  • Officials say drones can get eyes on a scene before officers arrive (Douglas County News-Press).

Risks and limitations

  • Drones, plate readers and AI tools bundled under one vendor widen the data police collect.
