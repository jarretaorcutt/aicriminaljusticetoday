Bangladesh's chief justice tells judges not to use AI to write verdicts as the Supreme Court trials speech-to-text software in 29 courts

Judges may use AI to search for legal references, but the operative part of every verdict must be written by the judge, Chief Justice Zubayer Rahman Chowdhury said.

Judges in Bangladesh may use artificial intelligence to search for relevant information and legal references but must not rely on it to prepare or write verdicts, Chief Justice Zubayer Rahman Chowdhury said on Saturday, the Dhaka Tribune reported on 21 Nov 2026. He said the operative part of every verdict must be written personally by the judge, based on their own judgement and legal reasoning. He spoke to judicial officers in Chittagong.

Separately, the state news agency BSS reported on Nov 21, 2026 that the Supreme Court has permitted trial use of a speech-to-text software called 'Judicial Assistant' in 29 courts and tribunals across 10 districts. It lets judges record witness testimonies, prepare orders and write judgments through voice commands, and converts PDF and image files into Word documents. A notification signed by Supreme Court Special Officer Md Mazharul Haque said the software was authorised for trial use "to evaluate its effectiveness."

BSS reported that the Supreme Court will hold an online training session for the judges on November 24, 2026.

What we know

  • The chief justice said the operative part of every verdict must be written personally by the judge (Dhaka Tribune).
  • 'Judicial Assistant' speech-to-text software is authorised for trial use in 29 courts and tribunals across 10 districts (BSS).
  • Judges can use it to record witness testimonies and write judgments by voice (BSS).

What remains unknown

  • How accuracy of transcribed witness testimony will be checked.
  • Whether the chief justice's remarks will become a written policy.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Voice dictation could speed up recording of testimony and writing of orders (BSS).

Risks and limitations

  • Errors in machine-transcribed witness testimony could enter the court record if not reviewed.
