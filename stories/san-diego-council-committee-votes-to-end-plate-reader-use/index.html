San Diego council committee votes unanimously to switch off the city's plate readers; the mayor opposes it

From the archive (Oct 21, 2026): The full council still has to vote, and the mayor could veto.

A San Diego City Council committee voted unanimously Wednesday to turn off the city's automated license plate readers and ban the police from using the technology, KPBS reported on October 21, 2026. The proposal came from Councilmember Henry Foster III; Councilmember Stephen Whitburn announced he had changed his mind and now supports ending the program. Roughly $2 million in funding for the technology remained in the city's budget. KPBS reports that for months last year the city's plate-reader database was made available to out-of-state and federal agencies, which Police Chief Scott Wahl called "an honest miss," and that in August, two men filed a lawsuit claiming the system led police to falsely arrest them. Mayor Todd Gloria opposes the ban and can veto a council resolution.
