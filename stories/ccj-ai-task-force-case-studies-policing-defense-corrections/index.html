National task force shows agencies how to decide on AI for police reports, public defense and prison classification

From the archive (Sep 16, 2026): The Council on Criminal Justice's AI task force applied its decision framework to three real-world tool categories.

The Council on Criminal Justice Task Force on Artificial Intelligence released three case studies on Sept. 16 showing how agencies can apply its User Decision Framework, first released in March 2026, the council announced. Each case walks through the framework's five decision phases, from readiness and risk through procurement, implementation and reassessment.

The corrections case looks at AI-powered tools that support classification decisions, including how to assess accuracy and disparities, preserve meaningful human review, and let incarcerated people challenge their classification levels. The other two cover AI-drafted police reports and general-purpose AI in public defense. Task Force Chair Nathan Hecht, a former chief justice of the Texas Supreme Court, said agencies cannot afford to implement first and ask questions later. The council says the studies are not intended to endorse any vendor.
