Utah prosecutors drop their plan to play an AI clone of a murdered woman's voice at a death penalty resentencing

Weber County told the judge it could not find a voice-cloning provider willing to work with it after ElevenLabs refused a license, KSL reports.

Prosecutors in Weber County, Utah, are abandoning efforts to use artificial intelligence voice cloning at a 2027 sentencing hearing in a death penalty case, KSL reported on Nov. 22, 2026.

The county had created a clone of Joyce Yost's voice by feeding snippets of a police interview recording into an AI tool from ElevenLabs, according to KSL. Yost gave sworn testimony at a 1985 preliminary hearing and disappeared before the rape trial; the man later convicted of her murder admitted killing her. A text transcript of her testimony survives in court records, but no audio recording exists. In past hearings, prosecutors have used human proxies to read her words.

In 2025 the county asked 2nd District Court Judge Michael DiReda to approve the AI clone for the upcoming re-sentencing, which followed a 2024 Utah Supreme Court decision overturning the second death sentence. On August 10, 2026, the defense brought two expert witnesses to argue the clone should not be allowed as evidence. After that hearing, ElevenLabs told Weber County it would not grant prosecutors a license to use its software, KSL reports.

The judge had not issued a final ruling when Weber County Chief Criminal Deputy Attorney Branden Miles told him on Friday that the county could not find another AI voice-cloning provider willing to work with it, so the prosecution is dropping the request. The re-sentencing is scheduled for September 2027.

No court ruled on whether AI-cloned testimony is admissible; the request ended because the vendor declined. This story does not name the defendant, following our policy on naming people in criminal cases.

What we know

  • Weber County created a clone of Joyce Yost's voice with an ElevenLabs tool from snippets of a police interview recording (KSL).
  • The defense brought two expert witnesses on August 10, 2026 to argue against admitting the clone (KSL).
  • ElevenLabs then told the county it would not grant a license; the county told Judge Michael DiReda it found no other provider and is dropping the plan (KSL).
  • The re-sentencing is scheduled for September 2027 (KSL).

What remains unknown

  • How a Utah court would have ruled on AI-cloned testimony; no final ruling was issued.
  • Whether other prosecutors are using or planning similar voice clones.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Prosecutors fall back to human proxies reading the transcript, a method courts have already accepted in this case.

Risks and limitations

  • Without a ruling, there is still no Utah precedent on synthetic voices presenting a dead witness's words to a sentencing jury.
  • A vendor's licensing choice, not a court, decided the question this time.
