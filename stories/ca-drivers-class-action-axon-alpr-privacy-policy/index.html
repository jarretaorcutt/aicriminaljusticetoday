California drivers file a class action alleging Axon's license plate reader network tracks them without required privacy disclosures

From the archive (Nov 12, 2026): The suit invokes California's Automated License Plate Reader Privacy Act.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 23, 2026
Story type

Jurisdiction
United States, California
AI application

AI framework

The complaint says Axon's fixed and police-vehicle cameras capture plates, locations and timestamps and make the data searchable through its Evidence and Fusus software, according to the analysis. Plaintiffs allege Axon, as both an ALPR operator and end user, failed to implement and conspicuously post a required usage and privacy policy. They seek to represent people whose data was processed by Axon from November 3, 2023, onward. The allegations are unproven.

What we know

  • Two California drivers filed a class action against Axon Enterprise over ALPR data (National Law Review).
  • They allege Axon failed to post a required usage and privacy policy under California's ALPR law (National Law Review).

What remains unknown

  • Which court the case is in and how Axon has responded; the analysis does not say.
