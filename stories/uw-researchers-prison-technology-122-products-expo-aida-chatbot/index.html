University of Washington researchers catalogue 122 products at a carceral technology expo, including an AI chatbot booth for incarcerated people

From the archive (Oct 7, 2026): UW News says the three papers cover technologies used in policing, in prisons and to monitor people after release.

UW News reported that University of Washington doctoral student Yael Eiger and professor Franziska Roesner published three papers on technologies used in policing, in prisons and to monitor people after release. The third paper catalogues 122 products presented at the largest annual carceral technology expo in the U.S., according to the page. One was AIDA, a booth where incarcerated people talk with an AI chatbot rather than with caseworkers. Eiger said AIDA's privacy policies were not clear. Eiger described the technology used by incarcerated people as inefficient, unusable, frustrating and expensive.
