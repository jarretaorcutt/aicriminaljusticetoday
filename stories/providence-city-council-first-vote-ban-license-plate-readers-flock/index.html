Providence's City Council gives first approval to an ordinance banning automated license plate readers

The 10-4 vote would force the city to turn off its Flock cameras and end its plate-reader contracts; a second vote and the mayor's decision are still ahead, The Brown Daily Herald reports.

The Providence City Council voted 10-4 on Thursday to pass an ordinance banning the use of automated license plate readers, The Brown Daily Herald reported on Nov. 20, 2026. The ordinance requires the city to "immediately deactivate and discontinue its use" of all plate readers, including Flock cameras, and to terminate all agreements with plate-reader companies. The city also could not acquire or use plate-reader data from outside owners.

The council must vote to pass the ordinance a second time. It then goes to the mayor, who can sign or veto it, the Herald reports.

The ordinance cites concerns including Immigration and Customs Enforcement use of Flock data, misuse of the data by authorized users and misidentification of people and vehicles. Ward 3 Councilor Sue AnderBois said the council was being given a false choice between safety and civil liberties. Police Chief Oscar Perez said plate-reader technology has become an important investigative tool for detectives and that the department would keep working with the council on privacy concerns.

According to Flock's transparency portal there are currently 60 cameras in Providence; the ordinance says over 100 are reportedly in operation, counting Rhode Island State Police contracts. A student report presented to the council in May found outside organizations searched Providence's Flock data more than 3.2 million times in a year. The city's three-year contract with Flock, signed in 2023, was set to expire in May 2027. In October, Gov. Dan McKee placed an indefinite pause on new plate-reader installations statewide.

What remains unknown

  • When the second vote will happen and whether the mayor will sign or veto the ordinance.
  • How the ordinance would apply to cameras run under Rhode Island State Police contracts.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Ends a data system that outside organizations searched millions of times, according to a student report cited by the Herald.

Risks and limitations

  • Police say they would lose an investigative tool used to develop leads in serious crimes (The Brown Daily Herald).
