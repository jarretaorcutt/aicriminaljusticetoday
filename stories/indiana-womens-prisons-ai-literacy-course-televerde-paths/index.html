An OpenAI Foundation grant will add an AI literacy course to a job program inside three Indiana women's prisons

The Televerde Foundation received $200,000 to build the course and an AI-powered resource center for graduates of its PATHS reentry program, Free Press Indiana reports.

The Televerde Foundation has received a $200,000 grant from the OpenAI Foundation to add an AI literacy course to PATHS, its professional development program for incarcerated women, Free Press Indiana reported in a story by Elyse Perry republished by The Republic on Nov. 12. PATHS runs in Indiana, Arizona and Florida.

According to the report, the foundation plans to use part of the grant for two things: a course teaching participants how to use AI tools responsibly in everyday life and at work, and an AI-powered resource center that would help graduates find information about housing, employment and community resources. Michelle Cirocco, the foundation's CEO, described the plans.

PATHS started in Arizona in 2021 and expanded to Indiana in 2023. It is a six-month program for women in the last two years of their sentence. In Indiana it is offered at Rockville Correctional Facility, Madison Correctional Facility and the Indiana Women's Prison. After release, a participant is connected with a life coach within 24 hours and then with an employment coach, Cirocco said. The program graduates approximately 110 women a year; more than 1,800 women were released from Indiana prisons in 2025, according to the Indiana Department of Correction figures cited in the story.

The article cites a University of Massachusetts Amherst study finding that Indiana PATHS graduates are arrested again at a 3% rate, about one-eighth the statewide average. That is the study's finding as reported; we have not reviewed the study. Shannon Barton, a criminology professor at Indiana State University, told the outlet that the bigger barrier for incarcerated women is not having enough access to programs at all.

What we know

  • The Televerde Foundation received a $200,000 grant from the OpenAI Foundation to add an AI literacy course to PATHS (Free Press Indiana via The Republic).
  • The foundation also plans an AI-powered resource center for graduates covering housing, employment and community resources (Free Press Indiana).
  • In Indiana, PATHS runs at Rockville Correctional Facility, Madison Correctional Facility and the Indiana Women's Prison (Free Press Indiana).
  • PATHS graduates approximately 110 women a year, according to the foundation's CEO (Free Press Indiana).

What remains unknown

  • When the course starts, and what it will teach in detail.
  • How participants will get hands-on access to AI tools inside prison, and under what security rules.
  • Who will build the resource center, and how its information will be checked and kept current.
  • Whether the AI course will be evaluated separately from the rest of PATHS.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Women released after long sentences may return to workplaces where AI tools are routine; a course could narrow that gap.
  • A resource center could make it faster to find housing and job information after release, if the information is accurate.

Risks and limitations

  • AI-generated answers about housing or services can be outdated or wrong; graduates would still need to confirm them.
  • The program reaches about 110 women a year, so access, not curriculum, may remain the main limit.
  • Reported outcome figures come from a study of the overall program, not of the new AI course.
