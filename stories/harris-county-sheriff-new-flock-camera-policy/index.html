Harris County Sheriff's Office limits Flock plate-reader searches to higher-ranking officers and active criminal investigations

The new policy takes effect immediately, months after three deputies had their Flock access suspended for possible misuse, ABC13 reports.

The Harris County Sheriff's Office in Texas has released a new policy governing its use of Flock cameras, ABC13 Houston reported on Nov. 22, 2026. Effective immediately, the policy limits use of Flock's database to higher-ranking law enforcement officers, requires searches to be connected to active criminal investigations and generally limits the number of license plates actively searched in the Flock network.

The change comes several months after three HCSO deputies had their Flock access suspended for possible misuse, the station reported. The sheriff's office said it developed the policy over several months with input from Harris County's justice innovation team, commissioners court policy advisers and other stakeholders, and that it also consulted criminal justice experts, reviewed model policies from national organizations and met with the team that drafted the Texas Department of Public Safety's automated license plate reader policy.

In a statement on Friday, Sheriff Ed Gonzalez said the office recognizes "the value of this technology in solving crimes" as well as "the importance of privacy, accountability, and public trust." The office called the policy a "living document" that will be updated as technology and best practices change. ABC13, citing Pew Research, reports that Harris County has more Flock cameras than any other county in the nation.

What we know

  • The policy is effective immediately and limits Flock database use to higher-ranking officers (ABC13).
  • Searches must be connected to active criminal investigations, and the number of plates actively searched is generally limited (ABC13).
  • Three HCSO deputies had their Flock access suspended for possible misuse several months earlier (ABC13).
  • The office says it met with the team that drafted the Texas Department of Public Safety's plate-reader policy (ABC13).

What remains unknown

  • The full policy text, including the exact search limits, retention period and audit rules; ABC13 did not publish it.
  • The outcome of the review of the three deputies' possible misuse.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Narrower access and an investigation requirement make each search easier to justify and audit.

Risks and limitations

  • Without a published audit schedule, it is unclear how compliance with the new limits will be checked.
