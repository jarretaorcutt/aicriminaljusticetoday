Plaintiffs appeal the dismissal of their lawsuit over San Jose police's warrantless searches of Flock camera data

The suit by SIREN and CAIR-CA, represented by EFF and the ACLU of Northern California, targets searches of nearly 500 cameras.

Services, Immigrant Rights & Education Network (SIREN) and the California chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR-CA) said on November 20, 2026 that they will appeal the dismissal of their lawsuit, SIREN, et al. v. City of San Jose, et al., which challenges the San Jose Police Department's warrantless searches of data from nearly 500 Flock Safety automated license plate reader cameras. Their attorneys are the Electronic Frontier Foundation and the ACLU of Northern California, according to CAIR's release.

Santa Clara County Superior Court Judge Jeffrey El-Hajj dismissed the privacy challenge in October, and the plaintiffs filed their notice of appeal to the Sixth District Court of Appeal, the release says. The plaintiffs argue that searching the citywide network lets police track residents' movements over time without adequate judicial oversight. The release says the department's Real Time Intelligence Center administers the AI-powered cameras, and that advocates warn the system could enable data sharing with federal immigration authorities, undermining California's sanctuary protections.

The account comes from the plaintiffs' own release; the city's response is not included in it. We previously covered San Jose residents' calls to end the Flock cameras after an officer misused plate-reader data.

What we know

  • The plaintiffs filed a notice of appeal to the Sixth District Court of Appeal (CAIR-CA release, Nov. 20, 2026).
  • Santa Clara County Superior Court Judge Jeffrey El-Hajj dismissed the challenge in October (CAIR-CA release).
  • The suit concerns warrantless searches of data from nearly 500 Flock cameras (CAIR-CA release).

What remains unknown

  • The trial court's reasoning; we did not review the dismissal order.
  • San Jose's position on the appeal; the release does not include it.

Risks and limitations

  • The plaintiffs say searches across the network allow tracking of movements over time without a warrant (CAIR-CA release).
