Mill Valley, California, suspends its Flock cameras after the company changes its terms of use mid-contract

From the archive (Oct 23, 2026): The city says the new terms lacked enforceable privacy protections; the cameras have been off since Sept. 9.

The city of Mill Valley, California, suspended its use of the Flock system after the company changed its terms of use halfway through the contract, Bay City News reported on Oct 23, 2026. The cameras have been disabled as of Sept. 9. The city said it had earlier reduced data retention to 72 hours for its live feed cameras and seven days for its plate-reader cameras and had restricted data sharing to select California agencies under written agreements. Staff plan to present findings and recommendations to the City Council in February, and any proposal to resume the technology would go to the council with public comment.

What we know

  • The cameras have been disabled since Sept. 9 (Bay City News via SFGate).
  • The city cited a mid-contract change to Flock's terms of use (Bay City News via SFGate).

What remains unknown

  • What the changed terms said.
  • Whether the city will resume, replace or end the program after the February report.
