New York bill would make judges who knowingly or recklessly cite AI-fabricated law guilty of misconduct

State Sen. Michelle Hinchey's Judicial AI Accountability and Integrity Act would let the judicial conduct commission refer cases to the Assembly for impeachment; she will push it in 2027.

New York State Sen. Michelle Hinchey, who represents the 41st Senate District, has introduced the Judicial AI Accountability and Integrity Act, the Daily Freeman reported on November 19, 2026.

According to the bill summary quoted by the paper, judges who knowingly or recklessly use fabricated legal authority in adjudicative writings would be guilty of misconduct in office, and the state Commission on Judicial Conduct could refer a determination of misconduct to the Assembly for impeachment. Hinchey's office said using AI does not constitute misconduct on its own; it becomes misconduct when a judge relies on a citation that is fabricated or materially false.

Hinchey's office cited a 2026 survey in which over 60% of federal judges reported using AI tools and at least 20% use them weekly, and said judges in at least three cases, in Mississippi, New Jersey, and Oklahoma, have acknowledged rulings with AI-generated fictitious citations. It also said AI tools built for legal research have produced incorrect information 17% to 33% of the time. Those figures come from the senator's release; the Daily Freeman did not give their sources.

A spokesperson for the Commission on Judicial Conduct said the bill was still being reviewed. Hinchey plans to advocate for it in the 2027 legislative session.

What we know

  • Sen. Michelle Hinchey introduced the Judicial AI Accountability and Integrity Act (Daily Freeman, November 19, 2026).
  • Judges who knowingly or recklessly use fabricated legal authority would be guilty of misconduct, with possible referral to the Assembly for impeachment (bill summary, via Daily Freeman).
  • Hinchey will push the bill in the 2027 legislative session; the Commission on Judicial Conduct is still reviewing it (Daily Freeman).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the bill has co-sponsors or an Assembly companion.
  • The sources behind the survey and error-rate figures in the senator's release.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Sets a clear line: AI use is allowed, unverified fabricated citations are not.

Risks and limitations

  • Impeachment is a heavy, political remedy for errors that may be better handled by court rules.
