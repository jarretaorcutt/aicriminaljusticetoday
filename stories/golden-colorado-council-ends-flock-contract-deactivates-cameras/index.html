Golden, Colorado, votes 6-1 to end its Flock contract and switch off its 11 plate-reader cameras

The council directed police to deactivate the system 'effective immediately' and stop sharing its data, and used a non-appropriation clause to skip the contract's final year, the Denver Gazette reports.

Golden has become the latest Colorado city to drop Flock license plate cameras, the Denver Gazette reported on Nov. 16, 2026. At the council's meeting last week, the City Council voted 6-1 to end the contract and directed the Golden Police Department to deactivate the Flock system "effective immediately." Mayor Pro Tem Lisa Vitry dissented.

The police department will also stop sharing data collected through the system, and the city's existing 11 Flock cameras will be removed in the coming weeks, the Gazette reports.

Golden first entered a contract with Flock in July 2023 for a seven-year term and was paying $27,500 yearly. Funding had been approved through July 2027, but a non-appropriation clause lets the city end its obligation; the council directed staff to remove Flock funding, saving the final year of payments and cancelling a two-year renewal that was set to begin in July 2028.

Police Chief Michele Wagner said the city started using Flock in response to an increase in auto-theft rates across the Denver metro area.
