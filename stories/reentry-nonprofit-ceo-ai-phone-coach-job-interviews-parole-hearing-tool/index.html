A reentry nonprofit says its AI phone coach for job interviews raised placements in an internal pilot, and it is holding back a parole-hearing version

The Center for Employment Opportunities' chief executive describes the tools in a Vital City essay; the figures are the organization's own.

The head of one of the largest US reentry employment nonprofits has described how the organization is using AI voice tools with people leaving prison, and where it is holding back. The account is an essay by Sam Schaeffer, chief executive of the Center for Employment Opportunities (CEO), published by Vital City on Nov. 18. Every figure in it is the organization's own, and the essay gives no method or comparison group.

Schaeffer writes that CEO dropped the idea of an app because asking someone leaving incarceration to download one, create a profile and manage a password "results in a 75% drop in engagement." Instead it built CoachLink, an AI voice coach people reach by telephone to practice job interviews. He writes that an internal pilot involving 500 CEO participants showed a 15% increase in job placements, and notes that phones can reach people before release because prisons and jails have them.

He also describes an AI voice tool for staff to rehearse difficult conversations, and says the group's motivational interviewing training produced a 10% improvement in competency scoring in as little as an hour. A separate job-training course on prison tablets has reached over 400,000 incarcerated learners, he writes.

The most cautious passage concerns parole. Schaeffer says CEO partnered with Project Commutation, an Oklahoma nonprofit, to build a voice coach that prepares people for parole hearings, many of whom appear without a lawyer. He asks whether board members might notice similar phrasing and grow skeptical, making outcomes worse. "Before deploying the tool, we are engaging attorneys," current and former parole officials and people preparing for hearings, he writes.

Schaeffer argues throughout that AI should add to human job coaches and not replace them, and he cites the roughly 600,000 people who return from prison each year.

What we know

  • CEO runs a telephone AI coach, CoachLink, for mock job interviews (Sam Schaeffer in Vital City, Nov. 18, 2026).
  • Schaeffer reports a 15% increase in job placements in an internal pilot with 500 participants (Vital City).
  • A parole-hearing practice coach built with Project Commutation has not been deployed while CEO consults lawyers and parole officials (Vital City).

What remains unknown

  • How the pilot measured the 15% increase and what it was compared with; the essay does not say.
  • Whether any corrections agency has approved the phone coach for use inside a facility.
  • When, or whether, the parole-hearing coach will launch.

Benefits and opportunities

  • A phone line needs no smartphone, app or internet, which the essay says is where many people drop off.
  • Testing a parole tool with board officials before launch addresses a harm that is easy to miss.

Risks and limitations

  • The results are self-reported by the organization that built the tools.
  • As Schaeffer notes, coached answers could make parole decision-makers more skeptical.
