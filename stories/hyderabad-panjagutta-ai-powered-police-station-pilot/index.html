Hyderabad police plan to pilot what officials call India's first AI-powered police station at Panjagutta

Five AI features, including CCTV search and facial recognition with mandatory human verification, are planned for the first week of December.

Deccan Chronicle reports that Hyderabad's city police have proposed AI-powered smart police stations, and that Panjagutta police station is being developed as what officials described as India's first AI-powered police station, with the pilot scheduled for launch in the first week of December. Police commissioner Mahesh Murlidhar Bhagwat visited the station and would take a final decision on the project, a senior police officer told the paper.

Under the initial proof-of-concept phase, the paper reports, police plan to introduce five AI-based capabilities at the central command centre before extending them to individual stations. They include automated scanning and retrieval of CCTV footage, AI-assisted facial recognition against authorised police databases with mandatory human verification before any action is taken, analysis of historical crime records alongside CCTV footage to identify repeat offenders and crime patterns, and real-time crowd headcounts and congestion alerts.

What we know

  • Panjagutta police station is being developed as what officials described as India's first AI-powered police station, with a pilot scheduled for the first week of December (Deccan Chronicle).
  • Five AI-based capabilities are planned first at the central command centre, including CCTV footage retrieval and facial recognition (Deccan Chronicle).
  • Facial recognition matches would require mandatory human verification before any action is taken (Deccan Chronicle).
  • Commissioner Mahesh Murlidhar Bhagwat would take a final decision on the project, a senior officer said (Deccan Chronicle).

What remains unknown

  • Which vendor or system would be used, and what accuracy testing has been done.
  • What rules would govern data retention and the use of historical crime records to flag repeat offenders.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Police say the system would speed up investigations and reduce administrative workload (Deccan Chronicle).

Risks and limitations

  • Using historical crime records to identify repeat offenders can carry past enforcement patterns forward into new targeting.
