Appeal argues Flock camera tracking without a warrant should void a 60-year sentence

From the archive (Oct 20, 2026): A Texas man convicted of five armed robberies says police needed a warrant to build a 'pattern of life' from plate data.

A 37-year-old Texas man sentenced to 60 years in prison after being found guilty of five armed robberies in 2024 is asking the Fifth Circuit to overturn his conviction, Mashable reported on Oct. 20. His lawyers argue that authorities used Flock cameras to create a "pattern of life" tying him to the crimes and that police should have obtained a search warrant before accessing Flock databases.

The trial judge denied a pretrial request to throw out the Flock data, according to the report. In a separate case, a federal judge ruled that using Flock databases to track a drug trafficking suspect's movements across state lines was unconstitutional.

What we know

  • The defendant was sentenced to 60 years after convictions for five armed robberies (Mashable).
  • His appeal argues police needed a warrant to use Flock data (Mashable).
  • The trial judge refused to exclude the Flock evidence (Mashable).

What remains unknown
