Pilot checks of the UK's AI probation note-taker found inaccuracies in 20% of records examined, the audit office says

The National Audit Office's Oct. 14 case study, recapped by THINK Digital Partners on Nov. 9, also says nothing yet checks the accuracy of the original transcripts.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 23, 2026

Justice Transcribe, the AI tool the Ministry of Justice and HM Prison and Probation Service built to transcribe and summarise probation supervision meetings, has been rolled out to approximately 11,000 probation staff, according to a National Audit Office case study published Oct. 14, 2026. THINK Digital Partners recapped the report on Nov. 9.

The NAO reports that a pilot with 40 probation officers between May and July 2025 found officers reported spending 73% less time writing up notes. But assessments by HMPPS's Performance Assurance and Risk group identified some inaccuracies in 20% of final case records they examined, primarily misspellings of names and agencies, and there were reports of the tool misunderstanding accents.

The NAO also says that at present there is no quality assurance mechanism to monitor the accuracy of the original transcript, which makes it hard to track the tool's technical quality or whether officers are becoming over-reliant on AI summaries. Officers must review and edit each summary before it is saved, and drafts are deleted after 30 days.

The report does not say how many records were examined. Our earlier story on the same report covered the rollout.

What we know

  • Checks during the pilot found some inaccuracies in 20% of final case records examined, mostly misspelled names and agencies (National Audit Office).
  • The tool has been rolled out to approximately 11,000 probation staff (National Audit Office).
  • There is no quality assurance mechanism yet to monitor the accuracy of the original transcript (National Audit Office).

What remains unknown

  • How many records were examined in the pilot checks.
  • Whether the error rate changed after the national rollout.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Pilot officers reported spending 73% less time writing up supervision notes (National Audit Office).

Risks and limitations

  • Errors in official probation records can follow a person into recall and court decisions if officers stop checking AI summaries closely.
