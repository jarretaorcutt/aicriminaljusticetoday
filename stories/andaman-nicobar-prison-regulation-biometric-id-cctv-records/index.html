India's Andaman and Nicobar Islands write biometric ID, CCTV and linked digital records into a new prison law

The regulation, published November 18, 2026, repeals the Prisons Act, 1894, and also sorts inmates by security risk.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 22, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
India, Andaman and Nicobar Islands
AI application
biometric identification, CCTV surveillance, computerised prison records

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands Prisons and Correctional Services Regulation, 2026, was published by the Union Law Ministry on Tuesday (November 18, 2026), The Hindu reported from a PTI dispatch. It repeals the Prisons Act, 1894, the Prisoners Act, 1900, and the Transfer of Prisoners Act, 1950.

The Regulation, comprising 70 Sections across 14 Chapters, mandates the use of biometric identification, CCTV surveillance, computerised prison records linked to the Interoperable Criminal Justice System and mobile jammers, according to the report. It provides for the classification of inmates based on their security risk and other criteria, and high-risk prisoners may be placed under close surveillance.

The framework identifies 24 prison offences. It will come into force on dates notified by the administrator, so it is not yet clear when the technology requirements take effect or which systems will be bought.

What we know

  • The regulation was published by the Union Law Ministry on November 18, 2026 (The Hindu/PTI).
  • It mandates biometric identification, CCTV surveillance, computerised records linked to the Interoperable Criminal Justice System and mobile jammers (The Hindu/PTI).
  • It provides for classifying inmates by security risk (The Hindu/PTI).

What remains unknown

  • When each provision takes effect; dates will be notified by the administrator.
  • Which vendors or systems will provide the biometric and records technology, and how the data will be protected.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Digital records linked to the national justice system could cut paperwork errors and delays in tracking undertrial cases.

Risks and limitations

  • Biometric data and linked records widen the amount of personal data held on people in custody, with no data rules described in the report.
