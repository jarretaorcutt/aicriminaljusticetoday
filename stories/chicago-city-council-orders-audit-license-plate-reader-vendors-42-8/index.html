Chicago City Council orders an audit of every city vendor that provides license plate readers

The order, passed 42 to 8, directs the Office of Public Safety Administration to check that vendors are not violating privacy laws or the state law that bars information sharing with federal immigration enforcement.

The Chicago City Council voted 42 to 8 on Wednesday to approve an order mandating the city's Office of Public Safety Administration to audit every city vendor that "maintains manages or otherwise provides automatic license plate readers," the Chicago Sun-Times reported on Nov. 18, 2026.

The audit is meant to ensure the vendors are not violating privacy laws or the state law that bars information sharing with federal immigration enforcement, the Sun-Times reported. The vote came the same day the council acted on gunshot detection technology.

What we know

  • The council voted 42 to 8 to order the audit (Chicago Sun-Times, Nov. 18, 2026).
  • The Office of Public Safety Administration must audit every city vendor that provides automatic license plate readers (Chicago Sun-Times).
  • The audit checks for violations of privacy laws and the state law that bars information sharing with federal immigration enforcement (Chicago Sun-Times).

What remains unknown

  • When the audit will be finished and made public.
  • Which vendors it will cover.

Benefits and opportunities

  • An audit could show whether plate reader data is reaching agencies it should not.

Risks and limitations

  • An audit alone does not change contracts or data practices.
