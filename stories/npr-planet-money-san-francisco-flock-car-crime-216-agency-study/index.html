San Francisco car crime has plunged. NPR asks how much credit Flock cameras deserve

From the archive (Oct 2, 2026): The one study cited found an 11% drop in car thefts across 216 agencies; it isn't peer-reviewed and didn't examine break-ins.

Car thefts in San Francisco were down almost 59% and car break-ins down more than 82% in January through August of 2026 compared with 2022, NPR's Planet Money found in an analysis republished by WVIA on Oct. 2. The only study it cites, a University of South Carolina analysis of 216 law enforcement agencies, associated Flock cameras with an 11% drop in car thefts in their first year; it has not been peer-reviewed and did not examine break-ins.

The piece also reports that out-of-state agencies ran more than 1.6 million illegal searches of SFPD's plate reader data, according to The San Francisco Standard, and that SFPD disclosed an internal audit finding 299 improper searches. Mayor Daniel Lurie announced tighter rules, including removing the city's plate data from the platform after 30 days.

What we know

  • Car thefts fell almost 59% and break-ins more than 82% vs. 2022 (NPR).
  • A 216-agency study found an 11% drop in car thefts; it is not peer-reviewed (NPR).
  • SFPD disclosed 299 improper searches in an internal audit (NPR).

What remains unknown

  • How much of the drop Flock caused.
