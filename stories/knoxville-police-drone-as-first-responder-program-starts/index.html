Knoxville police start sending drones to 911 calls from 12 docks across the city

The department says its policy limits flights to emergency calls and bars random patrols, with audits and a public transparency dashboard.

The Knoxville Police Department announced on November 17, 2026, that it is beginning a Drone as First Responder (DFR) program, with drones deployed autonomously from stationary locations to emergency calls for service. The program began responding to calls on Thursday, November 19, the department said.

Twelve DFR drone docks have been installed at city-owned facilities, and each drone can deploy to calls within an approximately two-mile radius of its dock. The drones are launched and piloted remotely from the department's Real Time Information Center. The project began in January 2026, when City Council approved an amended contract with Axon that included Skydio hardware and software, according to the release.

The department says its policy limits flights to emergency calls for service and as authorized in state law and prohibits random patrols or surveillance, and that flights will be subject to internal audit and documented on a public-facing transparency dashboard. Chief Paul Noel said the drones let officers get eyes on a call quickly. The drones will also be sent to fires, citywide emergencies, special events and damage assessment after severe weather. This account comes from the department's own release.

What we know

  • Twelve DFR drone docks are installed at city-owned facilities, each covering about a two-mile radius (Knoxville Police Department).
  • Drones began responding to calls for service on Thursday, November 19 (Knoxville Police Department).
  • Policy limits flights to emergency calls for service and prohibits random patrols or surveillance (Knoxville Police Department).

What remains unknown

  • How long flight video is kept and who can access it; the release does not say.
  • Whether any automated analysis is applied to the footage.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The department says drones can reach a 911 call within seconds and give officers better information before they arrive (Knoxville Police Department).

Risks and limitations

  • Twelve docks give the department wide aerial coverage; how well the no-patrol rule holds depends on the audits and dashboard being published (our inference).
