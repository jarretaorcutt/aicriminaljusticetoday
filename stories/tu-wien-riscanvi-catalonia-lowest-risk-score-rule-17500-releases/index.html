TU Wien researchers propose using the lowest score when equally accurate prison risk models disagree, testing it on Catalonia's RisCanvi

The study analysed more than 17,500 prison releases; the final decision stays with a human, the university says.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 24, 2026

Spain, Catalonia, Austria

A study from TU Wien, announced on Nov. 10, 2026, examines RisCanvi, a risk assessment system in use in Catalan prisons since 2009. Researchers Ashwin Singh of TU Wien and Carlos Castillo of ICREA and Universitat Pompeu Fabra found that models that are similarly accurate can rate the same person differently.

Their proposal is that when several equally reliable models disagree, the lowest value should be used for the person concerned. In collaboration with researchers from Catalonia's Department of Justice, they translated legal rules into an algorithm to determine whether people had reoffended in the legal sense, producing a dataset of more than 17,500 prison releases. The team says the rule did not cost overall predictive performance, and that technical measures ensure rehabilitative progress lowers rather than raises a risk score.

TU Wien notes the systems are mostly relatively simple linear scoring systems, not generative AI, and that in Catalonia both algorithmic and human assessments are made and compared, with the decision resting with a human expert. The findings were presented at the Ninth AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society. The university's account does not say whether Catalan authorities plan to adopt the rule.

What we know

  • RisCanvi has been used in Catalan prisons since 2009 (TU Wien).
  • The dataset covers more than 17,500 prison releases (TU Wien).
  • The researchers suggest using the lowest score when equally accurate models disagree, and say it did not reduce overall predictive performance (TU Wien).
  • The decision remains with a human expert (TU Wien).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Catalan authorities will adopt the lowest-score rule.
  • How often the models disagree in practice beyond the study's finding that they mostly agreed.

Benefits and opportunities

  • A simple rule could stop the choice of one model among several from raising a person's risk rating.

Risks and limitations

  • Prisoners can find it difficult to prove they were treated unfairly by a risk system, according to the researchers.
  • Risk scores rest on measurable characteristics and remain uncertain.
