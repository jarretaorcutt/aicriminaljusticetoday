San Angelo approves an $8.8 million, eight-year Axon contract that adds AI-assisted report drafting and drones

The council voted 6-0 for a package that bundles body cameras, a Drone as First Responder program and license plate readers, as the city moves off Flock.

San Angelo's City Council on Tuesday approved an $8.8 million, eight-year contract with Axon Enterprise that consolidates the police department's body cameras, drones, license plate readers and related technology into one platform, San Angelo LIVE reported on Nov. 20. The vote was 6-0 and the total is $8,798,046.75.

According to the outlet, the package includes $1.15 million for the Drone as First Responder program and $517,210 for fixed automated license plate readers. Police Chief Travis Griffith told the council it expands body-camera coverage, adds AI-assisted report drafting and enables live video streaming to the Real Time Crime Center. The department will move off its current Flock license plate reader system, which expires in February 2027, reducing fixed cameras from about 30 to 20 Axon units.

Griffith pointed to supervisor reviews, a civilian technology review committee and regular council briefings as oversight. Two residents spoke during public comment: one raised privacy and cost concerns, and another questioned relying on grants for the plate readers. No other bids were sought because the department is already under contract with Axon, Griffith said.

What we know

  • The council approved the $8,798,046.75 contract 6-0 (San Angelo LIVE).
  • It includes $1.15 million for drones and $517,210 for fixed plate readers (San Angelo LIVE).
  • The police chief said the package adds AI-assisted report drafting (San Angelo LIVE).

What remains unknown

  • What rules will govern AI-assisted report drafting, such as disclosure in reports.
  • Where the 20 fixed Axon plate readers will be placed.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The chief says the technology has helped locate suspects and reduce the number of officers needed on some calls (San Angelo LIVE).

Risks and limitations

  • A resident raised privacy and cost concerns, and the contract was not competitively bid (San Angelo LIVE).
