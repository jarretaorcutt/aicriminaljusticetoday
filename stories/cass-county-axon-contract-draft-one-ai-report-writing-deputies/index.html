Cass County approves a $9.6 million Axon contract that adds Draft One AI report writing for sheriff's deputies

Deputies and supervisors must review and approve every AI-drafted report, and the sheriff says officers who lie about reviewing one will be fired.

The Cass County Commission unanimously approved a $9.6 million Axon contract on Monday, Nov. 16, that adds artificial intelligence that writes reports for sheriff's deputies, InForum reports. The 10-year contract begins in 2028 and continues earlier agreements for body cameras, dash cameras, Tasers and other Axon technology.

The county currently pays $633,250 a year for Axon technology, County Information Director Bob Henderson said. The added software that includes Draft One would cost $366,636 a year, bringing the annual total to $999,886. Draft One writes incident reports using body and dash cam footage, and Henderson estimated it would conservatively save the office 7,072 hours, or about $339,456, each year.

Sheriff Jesse Jahner said officers and supervisors must review and approve the reports before they are sent to prosecutors. He said the technology inserts "catch phrases" that don't belong, such as a pink elephant, which reviewers should catch and delete; an officer who lies about reviewing a report would be deemed not credible to testify and fired. The package also adds a first-responder drone and four "dedrone sensors" to detect unauthorized drones. The Fargo and Moorhead police departments already use Draft One, and West Fargo recently approved it, InForum reports.

What we know

  • The contract is $9.6 million over 10 years, beginning in 2028, approved unanimously on Nov. 16 (InForum).
  • Draft One adds $366,636 a year, for an annual total of $999,886 (InForum).
  • Officers and supervisors must review and approve AI-drafted reports before they go to prosecutors (InForum).

What remains unknown

  • How the 7,072-hour savings estimate was calculated, and whether it will hold in practice.
  • Whether prosecutors and defense lawyers in the county will be told which reports were AI-drafted.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The county estimates the software would save about $339,456 a year in deputy time and put deputies on patrol instead of writing reports (InForum).

Risks and limitations

  • AI drafts can include wording that doesn't belong; the safeguard depends on officers actually reviewing every report (InForum).
