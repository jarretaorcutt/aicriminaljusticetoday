Henrico County and Richmond, Virginia, move to cancel Flock contracts as a study finds cameras clustered in Black neighborhoods

From the archive (Oct 20, 2026): The Appeal reports on the cancellations and a Hampton Roads camera-placement study.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type

Jurisdiction
United States, Virginia, Richmond, Henrico County
AI application

On October 6 the Henrico County Police Division announced it would terminate its license plate reader contract, and on October 13 Richmond Mayor Danny Avula said the city would cancel its contract with Flock Safety, The Appeal's Mike Ludwig reported. Avula did not rule out using readers from a different company. The article describes a study of the Hampton Roads region, co-authored by Steven Keener of Christopher Newport University, which found that about 80 percent of majority Black neighborhoods contain at least one camera; in Virginia Beach, police deployed readers in only five of 76 majority white census tracts. A state law that took effect July 1 limits storage of the data to 21 days, and a state commission found at least 21 police departments or agencies shared the data out of state, according to the report. The Appeal is an advocacy-oriented news outlet; we did not review the study.
