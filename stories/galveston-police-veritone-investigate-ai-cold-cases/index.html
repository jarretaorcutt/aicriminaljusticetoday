Galveston police add Veritone Investigate, an AI platform to organize evidence in active and cold cases

City council approved the one-year, approximately $75,000 package 5-0; the company says the AI organizes data rather than solving cases.

The Galveston Police Department announced in a Nov. 19 news release that it will start using Veritone Investigate, an AI platform, to help move active and long-running cold cases along, Houston Public Media reported. Galveston City Council approved it in a 5-0 vote on Nov. 12; the one-year service package costs approximately $75,000, according to city documents cited by the outlet.

Veritone CEO and President Ryan Steelberg told Houston Public Media the tool processes audio, video, images and documents and makes the material searchable. "The AI is not trying to solve the case for them. The AI is trying to organize the data," he said. Police Chief Doug Balli said in the release that entering one case would save the average investigator hundreds of man-hours.

Steelberg said the company works with about 450 agencies across the U.S. and that controls, audit logging and public records processes will not change. Houston Public Media reported that Balli did not respond to its request for comment.

What we know

  • Council approved the platform 5-0 on Nov. 12 (Houston Public Media).
  • The one-year package costs approximately $75,000 (Houston Public Media, citing city documents).
  • The department announced the tool in a Nov. 19 news release (Houston Public Media).

What remains unknown

  • Which cases and data sets will be loaded into the platform first.
  • Whether the department will publish a use policy or audit results.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The chief says it could save investigators hundreds of man-hours per case (Houston Public Media).

Risks and limitations

  • Bulk AI search of case evidence raises questions about who can search what, which the company says existing audit logging covers (Houston Public Media).
