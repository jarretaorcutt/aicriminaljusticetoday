Guardian and Bureau of Investigative Journalism find at least 25 court cases in England and Wales affected by AI misuse in 2026

One case involved a police officer who used Microsoft Copilot to write a victim personal statement for a sentencing; the court of appeal said no officer should do that.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 27, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
United Kingdom, England and Wales
AI application
Generative AI used to draft witness and victim statements and legal arguments
AI framework
Not yet labeled

The Guardian reported on November 26, 2026, in a story by Robert Booth and Mark Wilding, that undisclosed AI use has affected more than two dozen court cases in England and Wales this year. The Bureau of Investigative Journalism's trawl of published judgments, shared with the Guardian, found at least 25 cases with problems such as AI-generated witness statements, fictitious case law and nonexistent statutes. One criminal case involved an investigating police officer who, according to the Guardian, uploaded a transcript of a victim's interviews to Microsoft's Copilot and prompted it to produce the most tear-jerking victim personal statement to achieve the highest sentence. The Guardian reports that the court of appeal dismissed the offender's application to challenge a four-year prison sentence, and that the judges warned that no police officer should ever prepare a witness statement of any kind in this way. The paper says the officer was removed from frontline duty. It also reports that the Crown Prosecution Service had a lawyer cite an imaginary legal precedent earlier this month, that Law Society president Brett Dixon described how lengthy AI-generated case documents take up more of judges' time, and that the Civil Justice Council, in a working group led by Sir Colin Birss, recommended that witnesses of fact in civil cases sign a declaration that AI did not generate or influence their statements. A Ministry of Justice spokesperson said the government is taking steps to ensure any use of the technology in courts is safe, appropriate and properly controlled.

What we know

  • At least 25 cases in 2026 were affected by AI problems, according to a Bureau of Investigative Journalism trawl of published judgments (Guardian, Nov. 26, 2026).
  • In one grievous bodily harm case, the investigating officer used Microsoft's Copilot to write a victim personal statement, and the court of appeal warned that no police officer should prepare a witness statement this way (Guardian).
  • The Civil Justice Council recommended that witnesses of fact in civil cases sign a declaration about AI use (Guardian).

What remains unknown

  • How many of the 25 cases involved criminal sentencing or detention, beyond the case described.
  • We have not reviewed the court of appeal judgment itself; it is not yet reviewed by us.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Courts and regulators are being pushed toward disclosure rules for AI-generated statements.

Risks and limitations

  • AI-written victim statements can be built to push a sentence higher, and the Guardian says AI-generated filings are also adding to judges' workloads.
