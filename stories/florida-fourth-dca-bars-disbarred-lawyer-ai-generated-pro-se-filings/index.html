Florida appeals court bars a disbarred lawyer convicted of fraud from filing on his own after a flood of AI-generated documents

The Fourth District Court of Appeal said his filings included a hallucinated citation and called them AI-generated "slop." Future filings will be rejected unless a Florida Bar member in good standing signs them.

Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal has ordered its clerk to reject any future filings from a disbarred lawyer in his criminal case unless they are signed by a member in good standing of the Florida Bar, according to the court's November 18, 2026 opinion.

The opinion says the petitioner is a disbarred lawyer who was convicted after a jury trial of an organized scheme to defraud in Palm Beach County. After his appeals ended, the court says, he began filing AI-generated pro se documents and flooded the trial court with more than seventy documents since April 2025, according to the circuit court's online docket.

The court denied his latest emergency petition and found it contained a hallucinated citation to a non-existent case. It rejected his description of the fake citation as a "transcription error," calling it "an obvious AI-hallucination." "Abusive AI filing poses a serious threat to the orderly administration of justice," the panel wrote, adding that his response convinced it he would keep flooding the court with AI-generated "slop."

The ABA Journal reported the ruling on Nov 20. The order is not final until any timely motion for rehearing is decided. We do not name the petitioner, a private person in a criminal case.

What we know

Risks and limitations

  • Mass AI-generated filings can consume court time in criminal dockets that already face delays.
