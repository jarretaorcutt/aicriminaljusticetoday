A federal judge in Oklahoma ruled a warrantless Flock plate-reader search violated the Fourth Amendment

From the archive (Oct 2, 2026): 404 Media says the ruling will not set binding precedent.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 27, 2026

404 Media's Jason Koebler reported on Oct 2, 2026 that a federal judge in Oklahoma, Sara Hill, ruled that a Tulsa County deputy violated a woman's Fourth Amendment rights by searching her license plate in Flock's automated license plate reader system because it was a California license plate. The judge wrote that the search was not supported by probable cause and was done without a warrant, and described Flock's network as a type of indiscriminate mass surveillance. 404 Media says the decision will not set a binding precedent and that other cases are considering warrantless plate-reader searches.

What we know

  • Judge Sara Hill ruled the Flock search was an unconstitutional warrantless search (404 Media, Oct 2, 2026).
  • 404 Media says the ruling will not set a binding precedent.

What remains unknown

  • How other courts will rule on warrantless plate-reader searches.
