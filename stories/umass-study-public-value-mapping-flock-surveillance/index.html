UMass study offers cities a way to weigh surveillance tech like Flock cameras

From the archive (Oct 1, 2026): The research proposes "public value mapping" and examines facial recognition in Portland, Oregon.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 20, 2026
Story type

A study published in the journal Cities, with UMass Amherst public policy professor Thaddeus Miller as senior author, proposes public value mapping as a way for communities to weigh what a technology does against what else it may change, the university reported on Oct. 1. The study examines facial recognition technology in Portland, Oregon and autonomous vehicles in Tempe, Arizona.

The release says Portland's response to the facial recognition findings was to ban the technology in public spaces.
