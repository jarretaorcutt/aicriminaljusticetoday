Malaysia weighs China-style facial recognition cameras at crime hotspots

From the archive (Oct 14, 2026): Deputy Prime Minister Zahid cited a Johor pilot but said budget limits could block a national rollout.

Deputy Prime Minister Ahmad Zahid Hamidi said Malaysia is looking at expanding the use of high-definition CCTV cameras with facial recognition technology to crack down on crime, The Star reported on Oct. 14, after he met China's public security minister. Zahid said a pilot in Iskandar Puteri, Johor, helped lower crime and let authorities identify suspects and make arrests within three and a half minutes.

He said China installs hotspot cameras at intervals of about 50m, and that budget constraints could limit implementation nationwide. No decision has been reported.

What we know

  • Zahid said Malaysia is looking at expanding facial recognition CCTV (The Star).
  • He cited a pilot in Iskandar Puteri, Johor (The Star).
  • He said budget constraints could limit a nationwide rollout (The Star).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Malaysia will adopt the system; no published figures support the pilot claims.
