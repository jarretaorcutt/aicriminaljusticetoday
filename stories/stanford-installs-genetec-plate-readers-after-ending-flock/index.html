Stanford replaces its Flock plate readers with Genetec cameras; faculty critics say the approach hasn't changed

From the archive (Oct 20, 2026): Installation was about half done and the 30-day retention period stays the same.

Stanford University is installing replacement automated license plate readers made by the Canadian company Genetec, months after ending its Flock Safety contract, The Stanford Daily reported on Oct. 20, 2026. Luisa Rapport, the university's director of media relations, said camera installation is about 50% complete and that data retention on internal servers will continue to be 30 days. Stanford had partnered with Flock since 2024. The Daily reports that between September 2024 and August 2025, 20 public safety employees logged 1,742 individual searches in the Flock system across 129 investigations. Organizers of a petition against Flock that gathered more than 1,000 signatures told the paper it is unclear how much, if any, Genetec is an improvement.

What we know

  • Genetec cameras are replacing Flock readers, and installation was about 50% complete (The Stanford Daily).
  • Retention stays at 30 days (The Stanford Daily).
  • Staff logged 1,742 Flock searches across 129 investigations between September 2024 and August 2025 (The Stanford Daily).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Stanford will publish usage data for the new system.
  • Whether the faculty's requested town hall and committee will happen.
