British Transport Police's six-month facial recognition trial at London stations produced one alert, a false match, then was extended

From the archive (Sep 29, 2026): Records obtained by Liberty Investigates show the trial cost £320,786 across 18 deployments.

British Transport Police ran live facial recognition at London railway stations from February to July 2026, The Guardian reported on 29 September 2026, citing a freedom of information document obtained by Liberty Investigates. More than half a million faces were scanned, and equipment hire and staffing for the 18 deployments cost £320,786; the trial produced one false match. The force has extended the trial for a further four months and expanded it to London Underground stations, and said later deployments produced three positive confirmed alerts. The force said officers made arrests during deployments but that, as these arrests did not result directly from an LFR alert, they are not counted in its LFR performance data.

What we know

  • The trial cost £320,786 across 18 deployments and produced one false match (Liberty Investigates records, via The Guardian).
  • BTP extended the trial for a further four months and added London Underground stations (The Guardian).

What remains unknown

  • How the extended trial performed overall and whether it became permanent.
