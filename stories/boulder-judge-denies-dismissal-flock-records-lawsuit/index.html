Colorado judge lets a class action over Boulder police's Flock cameras proceed and rejects a contract-based records denial

The judge said a contract with Flock does not let the city evade its duties under the state's criminal justice records law.

Boulder County District Court Judge Michael Kotlarczyk denied the City of Boulder's motion to dismiss a class-action lawsuit filed in May against Police Chief Stephen Redfearn over the Boulder Police Department's use of Flock automated license plate reader cameras, the Colorado Freedom of Information Coalition (CFOIC) reported on November 19, 2026. The judge ruled on Wednesday, according to CFOIC.

The judge found the plaintiffs "have plausibly alleged" that the department's use of Flock violates their reasonable expectation of privacy by allowing it to "nearly perfectly track" their movements in and through Boulder, CFOIC reports. He also held that the department's contract with Flock does not "eliminate its obligations" to search for license plate reader records requested under the Colorado Criminal Justice Records Act, writing that a government entity "may not, by contract, evade its statutory obligations."

The order does not decide whether the department's use of Flock violates the Colorado Constitution's protection against unreasonable searches and seizures in Article II, Section 7; the judge called that "a question for another day," according to CFOIC.

What we know

  • The judge denied the city's motion to dismiss the class action filed in May (CFOIC).
  • He ruled the Flock contract does not "eliminate its obligations" under the Colorado Criminal Justice Records Act (CFOIC).
  • Whether Flock use violates the Colorado Constitution is still undecided (CFOIC).

What remains unknown

  • When the case will be heard on the merits.
  • Whether the city will release the requested plate-reader records.

Risks and limitations

  • The plaintiffs allege the cameras let police nearly perfectly track people's movements (CFOIC).
