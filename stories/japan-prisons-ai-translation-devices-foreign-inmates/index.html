Japan puts AI translation devices in 129 detention facilities so staff and foreign inmates can talk

From the archive (Nov 10, 2026): The Ministry of Justice handed out about 200 palm-sized translators. Only 28 interpreters serve the whole system.

Japan's Ministry of Justice distributed approximately 200 AI-powered translation devices in the summer of 2025, and they are now in 129 of the country's 171 detention facilities, AsiaNews reported on Nov. 10. The devices support about 90 languages, and the ministry chose a provider that guaranteed conversations would not be used to train the AI.

The system has only 28 interpreters. At Fuchu Prison in Tokyo, more than 20 per cent of 1,760 inmates were foreign nationals without permanent residency, according to the report. Staff told AsiaNews the devices help explain prison rules and give foreign inmates a way into rehabilitation and vocational programmes that the language barrier had largely shut them out of. Ministry data cited by AsiaNews put the number of foreign nationals in custody at 2,779 at the end of 2025, up 30 per cent from 2015.

What we know

  • About 200 devices were distributed in summer 2025 and are in 129 of 171 facilities (AsiaNews).
  • They support about 90 languages; the provider guaranteed conversations would not train the AI (AsiaNews).
  • Japan's prisons have 28 interpreters; 2,779 foreign nationals were in custody at the end of 2025 (AsiaNews).

What remains unknown

  • How accurate the translations are for legal and disciplinary matters.
  • Whether access to rehabilitation and vocational training has measurably improved.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Foreign inmates can understand rules and ask questions, staff told AsiaNews.

Risks and limitations

  • Mistranslation in disciplinary or legal conversations could have serious consequences.
