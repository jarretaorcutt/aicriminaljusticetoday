Chile's prison service tests an AI data system to help decide who moves to its education-and-work reintegration centres

Gendarmería's Biobío project, in its second round of testing with real data, aims to fill unused places in the Centros de Educación y Trabajo, G5noticias reports.

Chile's national prison service, Gendarmería, is building an artificial intelligence system to optimize the process by which people held in penal units apply to Centros de Educación y Trabajo (CET), the regional outlet G5noticias reported on Nov. 13, 2026. The project started in the Biobío region and, according to the report, has been validated nationally.

The outlet says the project carries a global investment of $460 millones financed through the Desafíos Públicos program of Chile's national research and development agency, ANID. Its stated aim is to fix the subutilización de cupos, or underuse of places, in these reintegration units and to ease crowding in closed facilities by cutting paperwork. It is in its second stage of testing with real data alongside the firms DataQu and Gotschlich y Neira Ltda. The current phase ends on 10 de diciembre, with results to be presented at two technical sessions before a final transfer to Gendarmería in 2027.

The platform will centralize information on people deprived of liberty to support the Consejos Técnicos, the technical councils that review cases. Robert Contreras Reyes, the regional ministerial secretary for justice and human rights in Biobío, said the tool lets officials assess more quickly and objectively who meets the profile to move to a CET. Colonel Jessica Rivas Hernández, Gendarmería's acting regional director, said CET selection is governed by Decreto Supremo 943 of the justice ministry and described the AI as an advance in data integration and profile analysis, presented through a graphical interface with comparison tables.

In the first half of 2026, 353 internos in the region applied from penal units to the centres, according to the report, a process reviewed by several multidisciplinary groups within Gendarmería. The report does not describe what data the system uses, how its outputs are weighed, or how its accuracy will be judged.

What we know

  • Gendarmería is developing an AI data-analysis system for applications from penal units to Centros de Educación y Trabajo (G5noticias, Nov. 13, 2026).
  • It is funded through ANID's Desafíos Públicos program, with a reported global investment of $460 millones, and is in a second test stage with real data with DataQu and Gotschlich y Neira Ltda. (G5noticias).
  • The phase ends on 10 de diciembre and the system is to be transferred to Gendarmería in 2027 (G5noticias).
  • 353 internos in the region applied to the centres in the first half of 2026 (G5noticias).

What remains unknown

  • What data and criteria the system uses, and whether people applying can see or contest its output.
  • How results from the test stage will be evaluated, and whether the technical councils keep the final decision in every case.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Faster review could fill unused reintegration places tied to education and work.

Risks and limitations

  • Profile-based scoring could entrench past patterns in who is judged eligible for less restrictive placements.
