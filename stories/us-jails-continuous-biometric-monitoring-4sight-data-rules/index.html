U.S. jails expand round-the-clock biometric monitoring of people in custody as data rules lag

From the archive (Sep 21, 2026): A vendor says 80+ agencies use its wearables; one county keeps the data indefinitely and Ohio jails can require the devices.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 20, 2026
Story type
Archive
Jurisdiction
United States, California, Ohio
AI application
biometric wearables, AI video analytics

Not yet labeled

U.S. jails are expanding continuous biometric monitoring of people in custody, Biometric Update reported on Sept. 21. 4Sight Labs says more than 80 agencies in 19 states use its technology and that it has monitored more than 70,000 people in custody; the outlet notes those figures are company-reported. Its OptiGuard platform applies AI to existing jail camera feeds, and in August it was extended to shared cells of up to four people.

Alameda County, California, approved a three-year, $747,000 agreement in 2025 for 100 OverWatch devices at Santa Rita Jail, and county documents said participation would be voluntary. In Ohio, use is not always voluntary, according to the report, and Ventura County said it keeps the data indefinitely until a person asks for removal or its contract ends.

What we know

  • 4Sight Labs says 80+ agencies in 19 states use its technology (company-reported, via Biometric Update).
  • Alameda County approved a three-year, $747,000 agreement for 100 devices (Biometric Update).
  • Ventura County keeps monitoring data indefinitely until removal is requested or the contract ends (Biometric Update).

What remains unknown

  • What federal rule, if any, will govern retention and reuse of the data.
