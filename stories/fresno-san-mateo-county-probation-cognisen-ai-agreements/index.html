Two California county probation departments have agreements with the same AI vendor, each with a ceiling above $3 million

From the archive (Oct 6, 2026): Fresno County's agreement with Cognisen runs up to $3,085,149; San Mateo County added a conversational AI assistant in September. Both county records say staff review what the software drafts.

Fresno County's Board of Supervisors approved on its consent agenda on October 6, 2026 an agreement with CogniSenseAI, Inc., doing business as Cognisen, to provide AI software to the county's Probation Department for up to five years, total not to exceed $3,085,149. The county record lists the action as "Consent Agenda be approved" with the result Pass, and Agreement No. 26-494. The department asked the board to suspend competitive bidding for the purchase.

The Fresno County record describes two modules. DocAssist is meant to cut the time staff spend producing reports, including reports for the courts; the record says Cognisen's preliminary estimates indicate AI-assisted report generation could reduce staff processing time by at least 50%. FieldAssist is described as using AI to prioritize individuals requiring attention, optimize travel routes, deliver real-time safety insights, and automatically generate case summaries and notes. The record says staff retain full responsibility for reviewing, editing, and finalizing all documents. It also says the agreement includes a non-standard limitation on liability provision.

In San Mateo County, the Board of Supervisors approved a resolution on September 15, 2026 adding Cognisen's PearlChat to the Probation Department's existing agreement, increasing the contract amount by $659,081.84 for a new not-to-exceed amount of $3,313,650.75. The county record describes PearlChat as a conversational AI assistant that lets authorized users ask questions about the department's case data, policies, and internal documents, and says it can draft travel permits, violation narratives and client correspondence. The record says the department began a pilot with the company in July 2024 and signed a five-year agreement in April 2025.

The San Mateo County record says PearlChat would not eliminate or replace human judgment, and that officers edit the content the tools draft.

Both figures are contract ceilings, not amounts spent. Neither record reports a measured effect on supervision outcomes or an independent check of the accuracy of the drafts.

What we know

  • Fresno County's record lists an agreement with Cognisen effective October 6, 2026, total not to exceed $3,085,149; the record lists the consent agenda as approved, result Pass, on 10/6/2026 (Fresno County record, File 26-0164).
  • The Fresno County record says staff retain full responsibility for reviewing, editing, and finalizing all documents (Fresno County record).
  • San Mateo County's board approved adding PearlChat on September 15, 2026, increasing the contract by $659,081.84 to a not-to-exceed amount of $3,313,650.75 (San Mateo County record, File 26-777).

What remains unknown

  • The vote count in Fresno County; the item passed on the consent agenda and the record shows no tally.
  • How accurate the AI-drafted reports and narratives are; neither record reports an independent evaluation.
