Hawaii advocates launch a campaign for a Clean Slate law that would use technology to find eligible records and shield them automatically

Rep. David Tarnas plans to work on the bill in the 2027 session; some prosecutors and public safety advocates oppose it, Hawaii News Now reports.

Advocates with Clean Slate Hawaii launched a statewide campaign in Hilo on Nov. 17, 2026 to pass a Clean Slate law in the 2027 legislative session, Big Island Now reported. State law already allows some records to be shielded, but only through a petition process that the coalition calls complex and expensive; a Clean Slate law would enable the use of technology to identify eligible records and automate shielding, with human oversight, according to the coalition's news release. Rep. David Tarnas, who plans to work with other legislators on the bill, said the issue affects more than 315,000 people in the state. Hawaii News Now reported that the proposal would make Hawaii the 15th state to automatically shield certain eligible records from public view, and that opponents, including some prosecutors and public safety advocates, argue that keeping records publicly accessible helps protect communities and lets employers and landlords make informed decisions. A state task force is expected to release its final recommendations in December, Hawaii News Now reported.

What remains unknown

  • Which records the final bill would cover and what system the state would use to identify them.
  • What the state task force will recommend in December.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Automating the process could reach people who qualify but cannot manage the petition process.

Risks and limitations

  • Automated matching across record systems can miss eligible records or flag the wrong ones without careful oversight.
