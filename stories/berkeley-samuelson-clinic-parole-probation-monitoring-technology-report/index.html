Berkeley Law clinic warns that AI-enabled parole and probation monitors are used to send people back to prison with little scrutiny

From the archive (Feb 17, 2026): A Samuelson Clinic white paper calls for procedural reforms and limits on incarceration for technical violations.

A Samuelson Clinic white paper from UC Berkeley Law examines surveillance technologies used to monitor people on parole and probation, the school reported on Feb. 17. It says next-generation electronic monitoring incorporates advanced sensors and artificial intelligence, which do not always produce accurate results.

Jurisdictions use these technologies to justify revoking parole or probation, with limited scrutiny of whether the technology works as advertised, the paper argues. It calls for codifying procedural reforms, technology training for decision makers, and limits on incarceration for technical violations.
