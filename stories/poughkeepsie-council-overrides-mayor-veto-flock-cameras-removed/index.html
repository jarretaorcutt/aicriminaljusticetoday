Poughkeepsie's Common Council overrides the mayor's veto, and the city's Flock cameras are being removed

Seven members voted to override Mayor Yvonne Flowers, who says the council acted without public hearings; police say the cameras were disabled Nov. 18, Mid Hudson News reports.

Flock cameras are being removed from Poughkeepsie, New York, after the Common Council voted Tuesday night to override Mayor Yvonne Flowers' veto of a measure ending their use, Mid Hudson News reported on Nov. 22, 2026. The council first voted in early October to discontinue the license plate reader system; Flowers vetoed that decision later that month, citing public safety concerns.

Seven council members voted to override. Council Vice-Chair Nathan Shook voted against both the original resolution and the override; he said he supports moving on from Flock by "letting the contract expire in January" and expects unintended consequences from the resolution. Police Chief Richard Wilson said the cameras were disabled Nov. 18.

Flowers said she respects the council's decision but is disappointed it was made without public hearings or a conversation with the city administration and police about how to proceed without a tool she said has helped reduce crime and gun violence. Council Majority Leader Nedra Patterson-Thompson said the decision "was not made in haste, nor was it made without consideration for public safety," noting that residents had raised concerns about mass surveillance for months before the vote.
