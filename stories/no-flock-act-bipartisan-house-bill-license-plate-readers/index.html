Two House members, one from each party, introduce the No FLOCK Act to limit license plate readers through federal highway funding

From the archive (Sep 15, 2026): States without limits on plate readers would lose 10 percent of some federal transportation money under the bill.

Reps. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) and Michael Cloud (R-TX) announced on September 15, 2026 that they had introduced the bipartisan No FLOCK Act. According to their release, states would be required to enact laws limiting automated license plate readers to specific public safety and toll-enforcement purposes; after a two-fiscal-year implementation period, states without those limits would have 10 percent of the federal surface transportation funding withheld. The sponsors cited a Washington Post investigation that found at least 69 police officials accused, charged with or convicted of misusing the technology. This is the sponsors' own release.

What we know

  • The No FLOCK Act was introduced by Reps. Krishnamoorthi and Cloud (sponsors' release, September 15, 2026).
  • States without plate-reader limits would lose 10 percent of the federal surface transportation funding after a two-fiscal-year implementation period (release).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the bill has had a committee hearing or vote.
