San José coalition demands an end to Flock cameras after an officer misused plate-reader data

From the archive (Oct 1, 2026): The officer was fired after a yearlong investigation; the city runs 474 cameras.

A coalition including SIREN, Working Partnerships USA and the Electronic Frontier Foundation rallied for San José to cancel its Flock Safety contract and audit its license plate reader system, San José Spotlight reported on Oct. 1. The rally followed the disclosure that a police officer, fired in April after a yearlong investigation, used plate reader data to help his cousin stalk a woman, according to the report.

In March, Police Chief Paul Joseph told the City Council that audits showed plate reader data had not gone anywhere it was not supposed to go. San José operates a network of 474 cameras. The council's rules committee has asked staff to analyze proposals to restrict access and expand audits; ending the contract is up to the full council.

What we know

  • A San José officer was fired in April after a yearlong investigation into misuse of plate-reader data (San José Spotlight).
  • The city operates 474 cameras (San José Spotlight).
  • The rules committee asked staff to study tighter access rules and audits (San José Spotlight).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the City Council will end the Flock contract.
