London Assembly opens an investigation into how the Met uses AI in frontline policing

The Police and Crime Committee will examine the AI tools in use, being tested or planned, and how the Mayor's policing office oversees them.

The London Assembly Police and Crime Committee is investigating how AI is being used by the Metropolitan Police in frontline policing, according to terms of reference published on 16 November 2026. The committee aims to understand the AI tools already in use, being tested or planned, and to examine how the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime (MOPAC) oversees the Met's use of AI, including future priorities and investment.

The Assembly's page sets out the context: the Government announced £115 million for police adoption of AI and automation in its Police Reform White Paper, including a new National Centre for AI in Policing ("PoliceAI"). In June 2026, Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley set out plans to expand the force's use of technology, including AI-powered video analytics, alongside the expansion of live facial recognition and drones.

In September 2026, the Mayor reported that the Met is limiting its use of AI tools to help complete "non-evidential administrative activities," according to the Assembly. The page also notes there is no universal definition of AI in policing.

What we know

  • The Police and Crime Committee's investigation was published on 16 November 2026 (London Assembly).
  • The Government announced £115 million for police AI and automation, including a National Centre for AI in Policing (London Assembly).
  • In September 2026 the Mayor said the Met limits AI tools to non-evidential administrative activities (London Assembly).

What remains unknown

  • When the committee will hold hearings or report.
  • Which specific AI tools the Met is testing or planning.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Public scrutiny of AI tools before wider rollout, including how oversight works (London Assembly).

Risks and limitations

  • Plans to expand AI video analytics, live facial recognition and drones are moving ahead while oversight is still being examined (London Assembly).
