Arizona police ran more than 110,000 searches of Flock's plate-reader database without paid contracts, AZCIR finds

At least eight agencies, including Phoenix police, got access through no-cost deals with the company or through other agencies.

Law enforcement agencies across Arizona conducted more than 110,000 searches of Flock Safety's license plate reader database without purchasing the company's cameras or holding paid contracts with it, according to an analysis by the Arizona Center for Investigative Reporting (AZCIR) published November 19, 2026. AZCIR analyzed roughly four years of Flock audit logs obtained through public records requests and compiled by the website Have I Been Flocked, and says the total is likely an undercount.

AZCIR found that at least eight Arizona law enforcement agencies, from police in Phoenix to sheriff's offices in Yavapai and Pima counties, gained access through no-cost deals with Flock or through other agencies. The Phoenix Police Department accounted for more than 100,000 of the searches, with an estimated 200-plus officers using the network under a no-cost memorandum of understanding signed June 21, 2021, that never went before the City Council, the outlet reports. A city spokesperson said the cameras helped solve missing persons cases and violent crimes. Phoenix chose not to renew the agreement this summer but continued to search the database for months afterward; the spokesperson said Flock was slow to remove access and has since done so.

AZCIR reports that Pima County ran at least 4,000 searches after Flock provided free access without a memorandum of understanding, and that Yavapai County, the Maricopa County Attorney's Office and police in Pinetop-Lakeside signed similar no-cost agreements giving search privileges for five years. The outlet notes that at least 11 agencies in Arizona have canceled Flock contracts and that Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes is investigating the state's use of license plate readers.

What we know

  • More than 110,000 searches of Flock's database were made by Arizona agencies without paid contracts, per audit logs (AZCIR).
  • Phoenix police accounted for more than 100,000 searches under a no-cost agreement signed June 21, 2021, that never went before the City Council (AZCIR).
  • Pima County ran at least 4,000 searches after Flock provided free access without a memorandum of understanding (AZCIR).

What remains unknown

  • The full number of searches; AZCIR says logs cover only a fraction of agencies.
  • What the searches were for; audit-log totals do not show case outcomes.

Benefits and opportunities

  • A Phoenix spokesperson said the cameras helped solve missing persons cases and violent crimes (AZCIR).

Risks and limitations

  • Access through no-cost deals can expand searches beyond an agency's jurisdiction with little public input or oversight (AZCIR).
