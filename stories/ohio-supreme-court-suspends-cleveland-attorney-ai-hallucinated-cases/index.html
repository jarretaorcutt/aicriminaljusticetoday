Ohio Supreme Court indefinitely suspends a Cleveland attorney who cited four AI-hallucinated cases and misled a federal court

The attorney confirmed using AI only when a federal judge asked, and owes more than $647,000 in judgments, including a $7,500 sanction.

The Supreme Court of Ohio indefinitely suspended a Cleveland attorney in a unanimous per curiam opinion, Court News Ohio reported on Nov. 24, 2026. The court found she was dishonest about citing four artificial intelligence "hallucinated" cases in a lawsuit against the city of Cleveland, and that she committed other ethical violations, including neglecting client matters and providing incompetent representation. She owes more than $647,000 in judgments.

According to Court News Ohio, the city told a federal district court that the attorney's brief quoted language that was not in the opinions she cited and cited four cases that did not exist. When the court asked for copies, she submitted 1,395 pages of case law that did not include the four cases. At a contempt hearing, the judge asked whether she had used AI to draft the brief, and she confirmed she had. The federal court found she used the AI-hallucinated cases and misrepresented the legal significance of seven other cases, and in October 2025 ordered her to pay a $7,500 penalty. She agreed to make $250 monthly payments but had not made a payment by her March 2026 disciplinary hearing, the court's news service reported.

What we know

  • The Supreme Court of Ohio indefinitely suspended the attorney in a unanimous per curiam opinion on Nov. 24, 2026 (Court News Ohio).
  • She cited four cases that do not exist in a brief opposing the city of Cleveland in federal court, and confirmed using AI when the judge asked (Court News Ohio).
  • She owes more than $647,000 in judgments, including a $7,500 federal sanction (Court News Ohio).
  • The federal court also found she misrepresented the legal significance of seven other cases (Court News Ohio).

What remains unknown

  • Which AI tool was used to draft the brief.
  • Whether and when she will seek reinstatement.
