A prison education nonprofit uses AI to read thousands of handwritten forms from incarcerated learners and is building an AI reentry tool

Level says the AI service DocuPipe has transcribed more than 5,300 multi-page forms, with staff and volunteers reviewing results. Its reentry and career tool, Carmen, is still to be detailed.

Level, a nonprofit that has delivered educational and workforce modules by mail to people in prison since 2020, said in a Nov. 6, 2026, post that it now uses AI to read the handwritten forms learners send back. The program has reached more than 7,000 incarcerated learners at 1,075 prisons in all 50 states and generated over 11,100 handwritten completion forms, Level says.

Volunteers had been transcribing the forms by hand, and the queue grew to more than 4,000. Level says it turned to DocuPipe, an AI document service, which returns a structured transcription of each scanned form in about 20 seconds at a cost in the neighborhood of 15 cents. Level says it found some mistakes, built a human-in-the-loop review by staff and volunteers, and that DocuPipe has transcribed over 5,300 forms to date. Forms are deleted from DocuPipe once they are transcribed, according to the post.

Level also says it spent most of 2026 building an AI-powered reentry and career support tool called Carmen, with support from Jobs for the Future's Entrepreneur-in-Residence cohort, and will share more in the coming weeks.

The figures are Level's own; no independent accuracy measure is given.

What we know

  • DocuPipe has transcribed over 5,300 handwritten completion forms from people in prison (Level).
  • Each form takes about 20 seconds and costs in the neighborhood of 15 cents (Level).
  • Staff and volunteers review the AI transcriptions (Level).
  • Level is building an AI-powered reentry and career support tool called Carmen (Level).

What remains unknown

  • How accurate the transcriptions are; Level gives no error rate.
  • How Carmen will work and who will be able to use it.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Faster processing means learners' coursework is recorded instead of waiting in a backlog (Level).

Risks and limitations

  • Misread handwriting could misrecord a learner's work unless human review catches it.
