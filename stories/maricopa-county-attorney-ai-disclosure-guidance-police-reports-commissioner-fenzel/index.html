Maricopa County prosecutors send police a template for disclosing AI use in arrest reports after two judicial rulings

A court commissioner declined to find probable cause in two cases where officers used AI tools to help draft narratives; prosecutors say they do not yet know what judges expect.

The Phoenix Focal reported on November 25, 2026 that the Maricopa County Attorney's Office has sent guidance to law enforcement agencies on how officers should disclose generative AI use in arrest narratives. An email dated Nov. 10 from Tom Van Dorn, the office's law enforcement liaison, acknowledged that prosecutors do not yet know exactly what judges expect and offered a template. The template says AI was used "solely as an administrative writing aid" for grammar, organization, formatting and narrative clarity, that all factual information came from the officer's own investigation, and that AI was not used to establish facts, determine probable cause or formulate investigative conclusions. The guidance follows two decisions by Maricopa County Superior Court Commissioner Daniel Fenzel. In a Gilbert case from an arrest on Nov. 12, in which the officer's report carried a note that it was generated using Draft One by Axon, Fenzel found no probable cause for a felony aggravated assault allegation and wrote that the weight of evidence for the remaining misdemeanor charges was limited. Earlier in November, Fenzel released a DUI suspect after finding he could not determine probable cause because the report was generated with help from an AI tool called Truleo. The charges are allegations. The Maricopa County Attorney's Office referred the Gilbert charges to the Gilbert Prosecutor's Office for review. This account relies on the outlet's reading of the court orders; we have not reviewed the orders themselves.

What we know

  • The county attorney's liaison emailed guidance and a template on Nov. 10 (The Phoenix Focal).
  • Commissioner Daniel Fenzel found no probable cause for the felony aggravated assault allegation in a Gilbert case where the report disclosed use of Draft One by Axon (The Phoenix Focal).
  • Earlier in November Fenzel released a DUI suspect after finding he could not determine probable cause because the report was generated with help from an AI tool called Truleo (The Phoenix Focal).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the proposed language will satisfy judges.
  • What the full court orders say; we have not reviewed them.

Benefits and opportunities

  • A standard disclosure could tell courts and defense lawyers when AI helped draft a report.

Risks and limitations

  • Reports that rely on AI without clear attribution can lose evidentiary weight, according to the commissioner's written reasoning as reported.
