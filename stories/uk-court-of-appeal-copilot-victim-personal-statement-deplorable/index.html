England's Court of Appeal says no police officer should ever use AI to write a witness statement after one used Copilot on a victim statement

From the archive (Sep 22, 2026): The court found the AI-written statement did not lengthen the 44-month sentence but called its use deplorable.

According to the judgment, the officer instructed Copilot to make a statement that would "make a judge or reader weep" and that met the higher harm category for sentencing. The court said no police officer should ever prepare a witness statement of any kind in this way and that no statement prepared this way should ever be relied on in a criminal case. It found the sentencing judge relied only on facts the victim had actually given in interview, so the deplorable use of AI did not lengthen the 44-month sentence, and dismissed the appeal. The officer was removed from frontline duties and is under investigation, the judgment says; the UK Police Law Blog analysed the case on 17 November 2026.

What we know

  • The officer uploaded the victim's interview transcript to Copilot and asked for the most impactful statement possible (judgment, [2026] EWCA Crim 1302).
  • The court said no statement prepared this way should ever be relied on in a criminal case (judgment).
  • The appeal against the 44-month sentence was dismissed (judgment).

What remains unknown

  • The outcome of the investigation into the officer's conduct in this and other cases.
