New York City's Correction Department moves to renew a $23 million Securus phone contract for Rikers Island amid AI surveillance concerns

From the archive (May 12, 2026): Securus has trained AI models on recorded jail calls, THE CITY reported; officials said Rikers calls would not be shared with ICE.

New York City's Department of Correction was set to renew its contract with Securus Technologies, paying up to $23 million over five years for phone service used by roughly 7,000 people detained on Rikers Island, THE CITY reported on May 12, 2026 (republished by Patch). Securus began developing AI systems in 2023 using its archive of recorded jail calls to train models designed to flag potential criminal activity, and one model was built using seven years of calls from the Texas prison system, the report said, citing the company's president. Brooklyn Defenders urged the city to scrap the agreement. Department officials said the agency has not enrolled in Securus' broader data-sharing network and that none of the calls would be shared with ICE. StateScoop reported on May 15 that the proposed contract was worth $23,235,000 and would run through 2031, and that the existing contract had been temporarily extended while negotiations continued.

What we know

  • The department planned to pay Securus up to $23 million over five years for phone service for roughly 7,000 people on Rikers Island (THE CITY via Patch).
  • Securus began developing AI systems in 2023 using recorded jail calls to train models that flag potential criminal activity (THE CITY via Patch).
  • Officials said the calls would not be shared with ICE (THE CITY via Patch).
  • The proposed contract was worth $23,235,000 and would run through 2031 (StateScoop).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the final contract was registered and what AI safeguards it contains.
  • Whether Rikers calls are used to train Securus' AI models.

Risks and limitations

  • Call monitoring can sweep in the families and friends of people in jail.
