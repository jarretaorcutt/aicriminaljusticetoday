Wisconsin Supreme Court dismisses a petition asking for statewide rules on generative AI in court proceedings

The Nov. 16 order cites procedural shortcomings with the petition and says the court will not further consider it.

The Wisconsin Supreme Court has dismissed a petition asking it to write statewide rules on the use of generative artificial intelligence in court proceedings, according to the court's order dated November 16, 2026.

The petition, Rule Petition No. 26-06, was filed on September 28, 2026 and asked the court to create a Supreme Court Rule chapter to regulate the use of generative AI by lawyers, judges, judicial clerks, research attorneys, court staff and self-represented litigants in connection with court proceedings. The proposed rules were titled as relating to "equal access to artificial intelligence in legal proceedings."

The court said it would not exercise its rule-making authority or further consider the petition due to procedural shortcomings with the petition, and ordered it dismissed. The order does not address whether Wisconsin courts should adopt AI rules in the future.

What we know

  • The petition was filed September 28, 2026 and dismissed by order dated November 16, 2026 (Wisconsin Supreme Court).
  • It asked for rules covering generative AI use by lawyers, judges, judicial clerks, research attorneys, court staff and self-represented litigants (Wisconsin Supreme Court).
  • The court cited procedural shortcomings with the petition (Wisconsin Supreme Court).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the court or another body will propose AI rules for Wisconsin courts.
  • What the specific procedural shortcomings were.

Risks and limitations

  • Without statewide rules, AI use in Wisconsin courts is left to existing professional rules and individual judges.
