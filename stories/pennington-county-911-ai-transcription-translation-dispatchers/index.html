Pennington County's 911 center uses AI to transcribe and translate calls while human dispatchers still answer

Director Kevin Karley says the center has used the tools for about a year and is researching AI screening of routine non-emergency calls.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 24, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
United States, South Dakota, Pennington County
AI application
Speech transcription, Machine translation
AI framework
Not yet labeled

KOTA reports that the Pennington County-Rapid City 911 Dispatch Center in South Dakota uses AI to transcribe and translate incoming 911 and administrative calls, and that the technology begins creating a summary as the caller speaks. Director Kevin Karley told the station the center has used the transcription and translation technology for about a year, and also uses AI to screen calls for quality assurance. "A human is still answering the phone," Karley said.

Karley told KOTA that the AI-generated transcript and summary are not the center's official records of a call, and that the center is researching whether AI could screen routine, nonemergency calls, a system not currently in use. KOTA notes the interview did not establish an independent error rate, specific time savings, or whether the technology provider can access or retain information from calls.

What we know

  • The center uses AI to transcribe and translate incoming 911 and administrative calls and to begin a summary as the caller speaks (KOTA).
  • It has used the transcription and translation technology for about a year, and also uses AI to flag calls for quality assurance (KOTA).
  • The AI transcript and summary are not the center's official records of a call, Director Kevin Karley said (KOTA).

What remains unknown

  • The system's error rate and time savings; KOTA says the interview did not establish them.
  • Whether the technology provider can access or retain call information.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Dispatchers can look back at a live transcript if they miss something a caller says (KOTA).

Risks and limitations

  • Transcription or translation errors in an emergency call could shape a dispatcher's decisions if not caught.
