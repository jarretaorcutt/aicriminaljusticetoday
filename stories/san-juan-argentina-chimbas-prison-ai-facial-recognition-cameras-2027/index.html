San Juan, Argentina, plans AI cameras with facial recognition at the Chimbas prison, aiming to have them running in the first half of 2027

The provincial prison service says the cameras will check whether workers and visitors are cleared to be in each area and send alerts to a new monitoring centre.

San Juan's Servicio Penitenciario Provincial is preparing an upgrade of the surveillance and control systems at the Chimbas prison, Radio D3 reported on 16 November 2026. Director Carlos Suárez said much of the new equipment will go to sector 5 of the complex, whose building is finished.

The plan includes security cameras with artificial intelligence and facial recognition to identify workers and visitors who are cleared to move through different areas. The system will be linked to a database and a monitoring centre that will receive alerts when movements fall outside set security parameters. Suárez said the aim is to have the new control centre and sector 5 running in the first half of 2027.

The plan also adds a third body scanner, bag scanners and metal detectors for visitors and for prison and civilian staff. The prison service is working with the provincial security ministry on cellphone signal inhibitors. Radio D3 reported that around 70 cellphones were seized at the prison so far in 2026, and Suárez confirmed three episodes involving drones this year; officials are considering drone inhibitors. The report is in Spanish; this summary is our translation.

What we know

  • The plan adds AI security cameras with facial recognition linked to a database and a monitoring centre (Radio D3).
  • The goal is to have the control centre and sector 5 running in the first half of 2027 (Radio D3).
  • Around 70 cellphones were seized at the prison so far in 2026, and there were three drone episodes (Radio D3).

What remains unknown

  • Which vendor will supply the cameras and what the system will cost.
  • What rules will govern the facial recognition database, including how long images are kept.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Alerts could help staff notice people in areas they are not cleared to enter (Radio D3).

Risks and limitations

  • Facial recognition errors could wrongly flag workers or visitors if alerts are not checked by staff.
