Oklahoma lawmakers heard a pitch to monitor people on parole and probation with AI and face or fingerprint scans

From the archive (Sep 11, 2025): The Frontier reported that a one-year pilot for 300 parolees would cost around $2 million. The House Public Safety committee chair said he remains unconvinced.

At an interim study in the Oklahoma House in August 2025, Global Accountability presented its Absolute ID platform for monitoring people on parole and probation through smartphones and smartwatches, with face or fingerprint scans at check-ins, The Frontier reported on Sep. 11, 2025. Company officials said AI would find patterns such as changes in places visited or how often a device is charged, and flag people for officer review for missing check-ins, visiting unauthorized areas or allowing their device to die.

CEO Jim Kinsey said a pilot for 300 parolees and 25 to 40 officers would cost around $2 million for one year. Rep. David Hardin, who chairs the Public Safety committee, said he remains unconvinced, The Frontier reports.

What we know

  • A one-year pilot for 300 parolees would cost around $2 million, the CEO said (The Frontier).
  • The AI would flag behaviors such as missed check-ins or allowing a device to die for officer review (The Frontier).
  • Rep. David Hardin, chair of the Public Safety committee, said he remains unconvinced (The Frontier).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Oklahoma has moved ahead with any pilot since the 2025 study.
