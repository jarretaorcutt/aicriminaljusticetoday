EFF: records show Sparks, Nevada, police plate readers use ICE's immigration hotlist, though the department lists immigration enforcement as a prohibited use

From the archive (Jun 25, 2026): Of 13 agencies that answered records requests, two had the hotlist enabled.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type
Archive
Jurisdiction
United States, Nevada, Sparks, Illinois
AI application

Public records show the Sparks Police Department in Nevada uses ICE's "Immigration Violator" hotlist on its license plate readers, the Electronic Frontier Foundation's Dave Maass reported on June 25, 2026. EFF notes that the department's own plate-reader transparency portal lists immigration enforcement among its "prohibited uses." The hotlist is populated exclusively by ICE, according to EFF. The group filed records requests with agencies around the country; of the 13 agencies that had responded, two had the hotlist enabled: the Sparks Police Department and the Blue Island Police Department in Illinois. This is an advocacy group's reading of agency records, and we did not review the records ourselves.

What we know

  • EFF says records show Sparks utilizes ICE's Immigration Violator hotlist (EFF, Jun. 25, 2026).
  • EFF says the Sparks transparency portal lists immigration enforcement among prohibited uses (EFF).
  • Two of the 13 agencies that had responded had the hotlist enabled (EFF).

What remains unknown

  • Whether Sparks police have since changed the setting or responded to the report; we have not checked.
  • Whether any stop or arrest in Sparks resulted from a hotlist alert.
