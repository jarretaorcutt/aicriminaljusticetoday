Quincy's mayor rejects the council's power to approve new police surveillance tech but keeps the rest of its ordinance

The council passed the surveillance ordinance 8-1; Mayor Thomas Koch struck one sentence requiring a council vote before the city acquires covered technology, calling it beyond the scope of the council's authority.

Quincy city councillors voted 8-1 to pass an ordinance covering surveillance technology, including automated license plate readers and facial recognition, The Quincy Sun reported, with Councillor Noel DiBona opposed. The measure came from Councillors Ziqiang Susan Yuan and Virginia Ryan.

Mayor Thomas Koch sent the ordinance back over a single sentence that would require a council vote before the city can "acquire, contract for, or begin using" covered surveillance technology, Mass Daily News reported on Nov. 21. Koch wrote to Council President Anne Mahoney that the requirement is "beyond the scope of the Council's authority." He approved the rest of the order, leaving the impact reports, the use policies, the public hearings and the limits on what vendors can do with city data.

The ordinance also covers some gunshot detection systems and software that combines data from multiple sources to identify, profile, predict or monitor individuals, The Quincy Sun reported. Councillors can override the veto with a two-thirds vote, six of the nine, Mass Daily News reported. The dispute grew out of the city's Flock cameras, which the mayor's office said in October had been deactivated, The Quincy Sun reported.

What we know

  • The council passed the ordinance 8-1, with Councillor Noel DiBona opposed (The Quincy Sun).
  • Mayor Thomas Koch disapproved the council-approval sentence and approved the rest, calling the requirement beyond the scope of the council's authority (Mass Daily News).
  • An override needs a two-thirds vote, six of the nine councillors (Mass Daily News).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the council will drop the sentence or try to override the veto.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The remaining rules require impact reports, use policies and public hearings for covered technology (Mass Daily News).

Risks and limitations

  • Without the approval rule, the final decision on acquiring new surveillance tools stays with the administration.
