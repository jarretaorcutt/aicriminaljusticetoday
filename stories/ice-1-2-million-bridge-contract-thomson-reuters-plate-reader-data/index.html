ICE moves to keep nationwide license plate reader access with a $1.2 million sole-source bridge contract

A November 12 federal notice names Thomson Reuters Special Services for a four-month deal; the current agreement expires November 30.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement intends to award a $1.2 million sole-source bridge contract to Thomson Reuters Special Services to maintain nationwide access to a commercial license plate recognition (LPR) subscription service, according to a November 12 procurement notice reported by Biometric Update on Nov 17, 2026.

The notice describes a four-month base period with two one-month options. A DHS acquisition forecast lists the incumbent agreement as scheduled to expire on November 30, Biometric Update reports. Thomson Reuters received a $22.8 million ICE contract in 2021 for license plate recognition services supplied through Motorola technology.

ICE is withholding the performance work statement as law enforcement sensitive, so the new deal's access permissions and restrictions are not public. Earlier ICE specifications sought a database receiving at least 30 million new plate scans monthly, covering at least 25 states, with alerts on up to 2,500 uploaded plates; Biometric Update notes those describe the earlier procurement, not confirmed current specifications. The notice does not establish that the contract has been awarded.

What we know

  • A November 12 notice names Thomson Reuters Special Services for a $1.2 million sole-source bridge contract (Biometric Update; SAM.gov notice).
  • The deal has a four-month base period and two one-month options; the incumbent agreement expires November 30 (Biometric Update).
  • Thomson Reuters received a $22.8 million ICE contract in 2021 for plate recognition supplied through Motorola technology (Biometric Update).

What remains unknown

  • Whether ICE's 2021 privacy safeguards on geographic searches and civil immigration use still apply.
  • Whether the contract has been awarded.

Benefits and opportunities

  • ICE says the service supports its law enforcement personnel across the United States (SAM.gov notice).

Risks and limitations

  • The work statement is withheld, so access permissions and restrictions are not public (Biometric Update).
