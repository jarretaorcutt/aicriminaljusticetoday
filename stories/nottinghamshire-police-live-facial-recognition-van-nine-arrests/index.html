Nottinghamshire Police report nine arrests from a facial recognition van, including two men wanted on recall to prison

The force also says officers stopped people who were not on the watchlist because they avoided the vehicle.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
United Kingdom, Nottinghamshire
AI application
live facial recognition
AI framework
Not yet labeled

Nottinghamshire Police said officers made nine arrests in Nottingham city centre after deploying facial recognition technology near the Winter Wonderland on Friday 13 November. The force published the account on 20/11/2026; it is the police's own description and has not been independently checked.

The arrests listed include a man wanted on recall to prison and a man recalled to prison and arrested on suspicion of burglary, along with arrests on suspicion of shop theft, burglary, fraud and drug possession. The force says the technology is built into a marked police van with a 360-degree dual camera system, that officers create a watchlist, and that the software erases every facial scan within seconds if no match is identified.

Detective Superintendent Will Henley, who leads the project, said the van "also works as a massive deterrent," adding: "Some people who were not on our watchlist avoided passing the vehicle at all costs and were therefore stopped by officers." That means some stops were based on a person avoiding the van and not on a face match.

The release does not say how many faces were scanned, how many alerts were wrong, or how many people were stopped without being arrested.

What we know

  • The force reports nine arrests from a deployment on Friday 13 November (Nottinghamshire Police release, Nov. 20, 2026).
  • Two of those arrested were wanted on, or recalled to, prison (Nottinghamshire Police).
  • The force says people who avoided the van were stopped by officers (Nottinghamshire Police).

What remains unknown

  • How many faces were scanned and how many alerts were false.
  • The legal basis recorded for stopping people who avoided the van.
  • The supplier of the system.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Locating people recalled to prison is a stated use with a clear legal basis.
  • Immediate deletion of non-matches limits retained data, if it works as described.

Risks and limitations

  • Avoiding a camera van is being treated as grounds for a stop.
  • No error or scan counts were published.
