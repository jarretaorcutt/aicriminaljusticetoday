San Diego's Flock plate-reader cameras are officially shut off, Flock confirms

The council voted unanimously to end the contract; vendors say they will delete the data at no cost.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 24, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
United States, California, San Diego

NBC 7 San Diego reports that a Flock spokesperson confirmed on Friday that San Diego is no longer part of Flock's network, after the City Council voted unanimously on Monday to terminate the city's contract following pushback from community members. Flock said in an emailed statement that all Flock services in San Diego have been deactivated and disconnected from the city's smart streetlight cameras, and that physical removal of the equipment is the responsibility of the city's smart streetlight vendor.

The council also voted 5-2 on Monday night to rescind Smart Streetlights authorization. NBC 7 reports that on Tuesday all San Diego Police officers had their access to ALPR data revoked except for two administrators still closing out the system, and that police said removing both the Flock cameras and the Smart Streetlights could take several months. Flock and Ubicquia, the Smart Streetlights vendor, said they would delete the data collected, at no cost to the city; police have asked them to send formal notification once that is done.

What we know

  • The council voted unanimously on Monday to terminate the contract; Flock confirmed on Friday that services were deactivated (NBC 7 San Diego).
  • The council voted 5-2 to rescind Smart Streetlights authorization (NBC 7 San Diego).
  • Flock and Ubicquia said they would delete the data at no cost to the city (NBC 7 San Diego).

What remains unknown

  • When the data deletion will be complete and confirmed; police have asked for formal notification.
  • How long physical removal will take; police said it could take several months.

Risks and limitations

  • Plate-reader and streetlight data collected before the shutoff exists until the vendors confirm deletion (NBC 7 San Diego).
