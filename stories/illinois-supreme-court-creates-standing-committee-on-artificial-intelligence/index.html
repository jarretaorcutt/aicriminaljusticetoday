Illinois Supreme Court creates a standing Committee on Artificial Intelligence with 18 members

The court says the committee will study safe use of AI in the judicial system and recommend safeguards, training and oversight.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 27, 2026

The Illinois Supreme Court announced on 11/24/2026 the creation of the Supreme Court Committee on Artificial Intelligence, a standing committee charged with studying how artificial intelligence can be used safely and effectively within the judicial system. According to the announcement, it will evaluate risks related to data privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity and compliance with court records, develop safeguards and ethical standards, and recommend training, oversight and governance measures. It will submit regular reports and recommendations to the Supreme Court, with administrative support from the Administrative Office of the Illinois Courts. The committee has 18 members appointed by the court, who can be reappointed for 3-year terms. The Hon. Jeffrey A. Goffinet (Ret.), previously a co-chair of the court's AI Task Force, is chairperson, and the Hon. Sara D. Smith of the Third Judicial Circuit is vice-chairperson. The announcement does not describe any specific rules the committee will issue or a reporting deadline.

What we know

  • The Supreme Court Committee on Artificial Intelligence is a standing committee with 18 members (Illinois Courts announcement, 11/24/2026).
  • Its remit includes data privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, court-records compliance, safeguards, ethical standards and training (Illinois Courts).

What remains unknown

  • When the committee will first report and whether its recommendations will be public.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The committee gives the court a permanent body to monitor emerging AI technologies.

Risks and limitations

  • The announcement does not say whether the committee will address criminal-case uses such as risk assessment or evidence.
