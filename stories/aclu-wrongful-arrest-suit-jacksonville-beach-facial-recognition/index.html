ACLU sues Florida police agencies for a man wrongfully arrested after an AI-assisted facial recognition match

From the archive (Jun 10, 2026): The man lived more than 300 miles from the city where the crime happened, the ACLU says.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 23, 2026
Story type

Jurisdiction
United States, Florida, Jacksonville Beach
AI application

AI framework

According to the ACLU, the facial recognition program incorrectly identified him as a possible match for a suspect in Jacksonville Beach, more than 300 miles away from his home. The suit names the Jacksonville Beach Police Department, the Jacksonville and Pinellas County Sheriff's Offices and two individual officers, and alleges the result tainted a photo lineup and that the warrant application concealed information. The case is ongoing; these are the plaintiff's allegations.

What we know

  • The ACLU and ACLU of Florida filed the suit on June 10, 2026 (ACLU case page).
  • Police ran a grainy image through an AI-assisted facial recognition program that incorrectly identified the man, the ACLU says (ACLU).
  • Defendants include the Jacksonville Beach Police Department and the Jacksonville and Pinellas County Sheriff's Offices (ACLU).

What remains unknown

  • How the defendants have responded to the allegations.
