Springfield, Illinois, renews ShotSpotter for $1.1 million as a request for more data fails 5-4

From the archive (Oct 21, 2026): An alderman wanted information on false alarms and sensor placement before the three-year purchase.

Springfield, Illinois, will pay $1.1 million for another three years of ShotSpotter gunfire detection, Capitol City Now reported on Oct. 21, 2026. An amendment from Ald. Shawn Gregory seeking more information about the system failed, 5-4. Gregory, who said he likes the program, raised false alarms and said the sensors are all on the east side. He and Ald. Roy Williams were the only aldermen to vote against the purchase. Gregory cited research in which, the outlet reports, the authors could not determine whether the technology reduced gun violence.

What we know

  • The city will pay $1.1 million for another three years of ShotSpotter (Capitol City Now).
  • An amendment seeking more information on the system failed 5-4 (Capitol City Now).

What remains unknown

  • How many alerts in Springfield were false alarms.
  • Where the sensors are placed; the city did not have to report it once the amendment failed.
