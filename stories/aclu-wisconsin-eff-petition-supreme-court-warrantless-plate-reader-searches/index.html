ACLU of Wisconsin and EFF ask the state Supreme Court to require warrants for searches of plate-reader data

From the archive (Oct 14, 2026): The petition names four cities and was filed for a church council and a pastor.

The ACLU of Wisconsin and the Electronic Frontier Foundation filed a petition directly with the Wisconsin Supreme Court on Oct. 14, 2026, arguing that police searches of stored license plate reader data without a warrant violate the state constitution, according to the ACLU of Wisconsin's release. The petition names four cities: Green Bay, Milwaukee, Racine, and Kenosha. It was filed on behalf of the Wisconsin Council of Churches, which the release says represents more than 2,000 congregations, and a Green Bay pastor. This account comes from the groups that filed the case; the cities' responses are not included, and the court had not said whether it would take the petition.

What we know

  • The petition was filed directly with the Wisconsin Supreme Court (ACLU of Wisconsin release, Oct. 14, 2026).
  • It names Green Bay, Milwaukee, Racine, and Kenosha (ACLU of Wisconsin).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the court has agreed to hear the petition; we have not checked later filings.
  • The cities' legal response.
