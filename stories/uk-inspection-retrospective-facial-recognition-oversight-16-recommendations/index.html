UK inspection finds police can't show how much retrospective facial recognition helps

From the archive (Nov 12, 2026): Forces make more than 300,000 search requests a year; the report sets 16 recommendations.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 20, 2026

UK law enforcement bodies make more than 300,000 retrospective facial recognition search requests per year, but use of the technology is not subject to consistent oversight and safeguards, an inspection found, Biometric Update reported on Nov. 12. Forces generally could not clearly show performance improvements from using it.

In a Home Office survey of 779 Police National Database users, nearly two-thirds could not recall any facial recognition training in the past year. The inspection makes 16 recommendations, including more data protection impact assessments by June 30, 2027.

What we know

  • UK forces make more than 300,000 retrospective facial recognition searches a year (Biometric Update).
  • Nearly two-thirds of 779 surveyed database users could not recall recent training (Biometric Update).
  • The inspection sets 16 recommendations (Biometric Update).

What remains unknown

  • Whether bias in the algorithm contributed to any wrongful arrests.
