Sweden's prison and probation service lets the public practise talking with "AI-Kai," a digital prisoner, to recruit staff

Kriminalvården says the tool is not used to assess or select candidates, SVT reports.

Sweden's prison and probation service, Kriminalvården, needs to recruit thousands of new staff in the coming years and has built an AI tool called AI-Kai to give the public a look at the work, SVT Nyheter Öst reported on 23 November 2026. Kai is a digital prisoner designed to mimic the kinds of conversations correctional officers can face.

Kai responds in real time and adapts to how questions are asked, according to SVT; after four minutes the user gets an evaluation. Deputy HR director Sofia Palmblad told SVT that the reporter's open, exploratory questions helped Kai relax. She stressed that the tool is not used to assess or select candidates in recruitment.

SVT lists Stockholm, Norrköping, Umeå, Göteborg and Kalmar as cities where people can try it. The SVT report is in Swedish; this summary is our translation.

What we know

  • Kriminalvården built AI-Kai, a digital prisoner that mimics officer conversations (SVT).
  • The tool gives feedback after four minutes and is not used to assess or select candidates, according to Sofia Palmblad (SVT).
  • The public can try it in Stockholm, Norrköping, Umeå, Göteborg and Kalmar (SVT).

What remains unknown

  • What model or vendor powers AI-Kai and what conversation data it keeps.
  • Whether the agency plans to use the tool in staff training.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Gives prospective staff a realistic preview of de-escalation conversations before they apply.

Risks and limitations

  • A simulated prisoner may reduce real incarcerated people to stereotypes if its persona is poorly designed.
