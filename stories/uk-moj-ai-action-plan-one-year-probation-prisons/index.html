UK justice ministry: AI note-taking reaches 12,000 probation officers, and predictive tools are live in prisons and probation

From the archive (Sep 24, 2026): The Ministry of Justice's one-year update to its AI Action Plan names a prison violence estimator, GPS crime mapping for people on probation, and facial recognition check-ins.

The UK Ministry of Justice published a one-year update to its AI Action Plan for Justice on Sept. 24. It says Justice Transcribe, the AI tool that transcribes and summarizes probation supervision sessions, has been scaled to over 12,000 probation officers, and that over 1.5 million meetings were summarized between Oct. 7, 2025 and Sept. 14, 2026. The ministry says it is exploring extending the tool to other parts of the justice system, including courts.

The update also lists non-generative tools it says are now live across probation, prisons and courts. They include a Violence in Prisons Estimator, which uses predictive analytics to assess the risk of involvement in violent incidents in custody; Acquisitive Crime Mapping, which matches GPS location data of people on probation with police crime data; and online probation check-ins that use facial recognition to verify identity.

The ministry describes a Digital Rapid Response Unit building AI-enabled tools to help prevent people being released from prison in error. It also adds a fourth priority to the plan: responding to emerging AI risks, including AI-enabled crime. The update is the ministry's own account; it does not include independent evaluation of the tools' accuracy.

What we know

  • Justice Transcribe has been scaled to over 12,000 probation officers (MoJ).
  • Over 1.5 million meetings were summarized between Oct. 7, 2025 and Sept. 14, 2026 (MoJ).
  • A Violence in Prisons Estimator, Acquisitive Crime Mapping and facial recognition probation check-ins are listed as live (MoJ).
  • A fourth priority on AI risks, including AI-enabled criminality, has been added (MoJ).

What remains unknown

  • How accurate the violence estimator is, and how its scores are used in prison management.
  • What safeguards apply when GPS data from people on probation is matched against police crime data.
  • Error rates of facial recognition check-ins, and what happens when a check-in fails.
  • Any independent evaluation; this is the ministry's own report.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Less time on paperwork for probation officers, according to the ministry.
  • Tools aimed at preventing release in error, according to the ministry.

Risks and limitations

  • Predictive scores and location matching can shape how people in custody and on probation are treated, with little public evidence on accuracy.
  • Biometric check-in failures could be read as non-compliance.
