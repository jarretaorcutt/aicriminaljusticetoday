Alexandria City Council weighs rules that would require its approval before police use any surveillance technology

The proposal from Police Chief Tarrick McGuire follows the city's decision to switch off all 61 of its license plate reader cameras, and would not turn them back on.

The Alexandria City Council will consider new rules for future police surveillance technology at its 7 p.m. meeting on Tuesday, FOX 5 DC reported on Nov. 24, 2026.

The proposal from Police Chief Tarrick McGuire would require City Council approval before Alexandria police could use surveillance equipment. For automated license plate readers, police would also have to publish an impact report and a policy explaining how the technology would be used, available at least 21 days before a public hearing, according to FOX 5.

The council would consider whether a technology's benefits outweigh its costs and whether civil rights are protected. Approved systems could be authorized for up to five years, with an annual report on costs, locations, use and data shared outside the department. An emergency exception would let police use surveillance technology temporarily to respond to an urgent threat to life or property, with time limits and a report to the council afterward, FOX 5 reported.

The proposal follows the city's decision to deactivate all 61 of its automated license plate reader cameras. The city says the fixed readers were decommissioned Oct. 30 and are no longer collecting data. The proposal would not turn those cameras back on, FOX 5 reported.
