Tulane researchers turn New Orleans court dockets into trackable case histories and find detained defendants plead guilty more often

A preprint describes software that reads clerk notes from 130,640 cases; the authors say the patterns need more context before conclusions are drawn.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type
Research
Jurisdiction
United States, Louisiana, New Orleans
AI application
natural language processing, court data analytics
AI framework
Not yet labeled

Researchers at Tulane University have built software that converts free-text court clerk notes from New Orleans into structured case histories, according to a preprint posted to arXiv and dated 17 Nov 2026. The paper has not been peer reviewed.

The team collected 130,640 criminal cases containing 820,564 proceedings from the Orleans Parish Docket Master portal. Rule-based extractors sort the clerk entries into twenty-one procedural labels, and a text-similarity model is used to find comparable cases. The researchers built an interactive dashboard on top of the data and report a small, preliminary study with community partners, including Court Watch NOLA.

The paper reports several patterns. From 2022 to 2026, 77.4% of detained defendants pleaded guilty, against 56.8% of cases where the defendant was not detained. Cases involving Black defendants ran approximately 16.1% longer than those involving white defendants, an average of 90 days compared with 77.5 days. Single-charge cases were dropped 27.1% of the time, while cases with two charges were dismissed 9.0% of the time. The system also flags possible violations of Louisiana's Article 701 time limits for people held in custody.

The authors add cautions. Pre-trial custody is not a recorded field and had to be inferred, the dataset is a captured subset of public dockets and not an official archive, and they write that more case context, such as charges and history, is needed to interpret the disparities. The study covers one jurisdiction.

What we know

  • The dataset covers 130,640 cases and 820,564 proceedings from Orleans Parish (arXiv preprint, Nov. 17, 2026).
  • 77.4% of detained defendants pleaded guilty from 2022 to 2026, against 56.8% of those not detained (arXiv preprint).
  • Cases involving Black defendants ran approximately 16.1% longer on average (arXiv preprint).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the plea and duration gaps hold after accounting for charge type and criminal history; the authors say more context is required.
  • How many of the flagged Article 701 cases are actual violations.
  • Whether courts or public defenders in New Orleans are using the dashboard.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Structured docket data lets court watchers and defenders see delays and detention patterns that are buried in clerk notes.
  • Rule-based extraction is easier to audit than a black-box model.

Risks and limitations

  • Inferred custody status and an incomplete docket sample could mislead if read as official statistics.
  • Raw disparities can be misread as proof of cause.

Sources and primary documents
