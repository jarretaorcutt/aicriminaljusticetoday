Santa Ana City Council votes unanimously to end its Flock and Motorola plate-reader agreements

The council also asked staff to look into the legality of banning such cameras on private property.

The Santa Ana City Council unanimously voted on Tuesday, Nov. 17 to terminate current agreements with Flock Group, Inc. and Motorola Solutions, Inc. for the Santa Ana Police Department's automated license plate reader (ALPR) program, the City of Santa Ana said in a news release posted November 18, 2026.

The council directed the City Manager to terminate the agreements and directed staff to look into the legality of potential bans of such cameras on private property. According to the city, the council cited concerns about residents' privacy and potential sharing of data by the two companies with non-City agencies or entities.

The city said that since use of the ALPR system began in September 2025, it has not shared ALPR data with law enforcement agencies outside of California, with any federal law enforcement agencies, or for immigration enforcement.

What we know

  • The council voted unanimously on Nov. 17 to terminate the city's ALPR agreements with Flock Group, Inc. and Motorola Solutions, Inc. (City of Santa Ana).

  • The city says it has not shared ALPR data outside of California, with federal agencies or for immigration enforcement since September 2025 (City of Santa Ana).

What remains unknown

  • When the cameras will be switched off and whether the city will replace them.
  • Any costs of ending the agreements; the release gives none.
