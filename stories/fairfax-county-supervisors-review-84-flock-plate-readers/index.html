Fairfax County supervisors spend two hours on the future of 84 Flock plate readers and reach no decision

From the archive (Oct 21, 2026): Police cited 938 arrests since 2022; several supervisors said privacy questions went unanswered.

The Fairfax County Board of Supervisors' Safety and Security Committee, chaired by Supervisor Rodney Lusk, spent two hours on Oct. 20 on the future of automatic license plate readers, FFXnow reported. County police operate 84 readers procured from Flock Safety and have access to data from approximately 120 more privately owned cameras. A 2025 state law limits storage of the data to a maximum of 21 days. Police reported 938 arrests with a total of 2,672 charges since November 2022 and said the number of personnel with access was cut from 821 to 296. The board had put future installations on hold the month before, and the county School Board voted on Sept. 24 to ban the readers from school property, according to the report.

What we know

  • County police operate 84 Flock readers and can access about 120 more privately owned cameras (FFXnow).
  • Police reported 938 arrests with 2,672 charges since November 2022 (FFXnow).
  • No decision was reached; staff laid out scenarios and supervisors were to send questions (FFXnow).

What remains unknown

  • Which option the board will choose.
  • Whether Flock's written answers to the board satisfied supervisors.
