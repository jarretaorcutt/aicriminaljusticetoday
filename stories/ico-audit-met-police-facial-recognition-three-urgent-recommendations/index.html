UK privacy regulator's audit of the Met Police's facial recognition makes 29 recommendations, three of them urgent

The Information Commissioner's Office rated both live and retrospective facial recognition 'Reasonable,' but every urgent item concerns retrospective searches, including how long probe images are kept.

The UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has published the executive summary of a data protection audit of the Metropolitan Police Service's facial recognition use; the ICO's audit page is dated 20 November 2026. The Met agreed to the audit, which covered live facial recognition (LFR) and retrospective facial recognition (RFR), the searching of already-captured images against police photo databases.

The ICO rated both areas 'Reasonable,' meaning processes are in place but there is scope for improvement. For live use it made five high, five medium and one low priority recommendation. For retrospective use it made three urgent, 11 high and four medium priority recommendations. Biometric Update counts 29 recommendations in all.

On retrospective searches, the audit found the Data Protection Officer appears detached from oversight, and that staff described searching the Police National Database only when the Met's internal Reveal system fails to find a match, a process that is not appropriately documented. Auditors could not gain assurance that officers handling probe images keep them in line with the relevant retention periods, and the review before deletion is a manual process. On live use, training completion is not formally documented and staff have not had refresher training.

The ICO also found areas of assurance, including comprehensive LFR policies and regular testing of the fairness and accuracy of LFR and of Reveal. The audit did not assess the Met's planned Operator Initiated Facial Recognition, which was not operational at the time.

What we know

  • The ICO gave both live and retrospective facial recognition a 'Reasonable' assurance rating (ICO audit executive summary).
  • Retrospective facial recognition drew three urgent, 11 high and four medium priority recommendations (ICO audit executive summary).
  • Auditors could not gain assurance that probe images are kept in line with retention periods (ICO audit executive summary).
  • The ICO's audit page is dated 20 November 2026 and says the audit was carried out with the Met's consent (ICO).

What remains unknown

  • When the Met will complete the urgent recommendations.
  • Whether any probe images were actually kept beyond their retention periods.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Publishes a regulator's specific findings on how the Met governs facial recognition searches.

Risks and limitations

  • The ICO warns undocumented searches of the Police National Database could run without appropriate oversight (ICO audit executive summary).
