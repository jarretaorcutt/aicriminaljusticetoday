European Court of Human Rights bars a Romanian lawyer for two years over unverified AI-generated citations

The court says her filing cited non-existent cases and fictitious paragraphs in real judgments. She acknowledged using AI tools to draft it.

The European Court of Human Rights has excluded a Romanian lawyer from representing or otherwise assisting litigants before it for two years, according to a press release issued by the Registrar of the Court (ECHR 290 (2026), dated 18.11.2026). The President of the Court made the decision on 13 November 2026 under Rule 44D of the Rules of Court, following a reasoned proposal by a Chamber. The release does not name the lawyer.

The Chamber found that observations she submitted in a case contained numerous errors about the Court's case-law: citations of non-existent ("hallucinated") cases, citations of fictitious paragraphs in genuine judgments and decisions, and citations of real case-law that was irrelevant to, or plainly contradicted, the points it was meant to support. The lawyer acknowledged that she had used Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools to draft the observations and described the mistakes as "inaccuracies" and "citation errors", the release says.

The Chamber said she should have independently checked all AI-generated references against authoritative sources such as the Court's case-law database, HUDOC, and that for certain references no checks whatsoever had been carried out. It concluded her conduct had caused prejudice to the administration of justice, because the Court had to spend considerable time verifying the observations.

The Chamber considered comments from the lawyer, the Romanian Government and the Romanian National Bar Association. The exclusion concerns only one of the two representatives in the case, and the decision does not prejudice the possibility of her rights of representation being restored under Rule 44D, the release says. The release notes that it is a Registry document and does not bind the Court.

What we know

  • The President of the Court decided on 13 November 2026 to exclude the lawyer for two years under Rule 44D (ECHR press release ECHR 290 (2026), 18.11.2026).
  • Her observations cited non-existent ("hallucinated") cases and fictitious paragraphs in genuine judgments (ECHR press release).
  • She acknowledged using AI tools to draft the observations (ECHR press release).
  • The exclusion concerns only one of the two representatives in the case (ECHR press release).

What remains unknown

  • The lawyer's name and the case involved; the release gives neither.
  • Which AI tools she used.
  • Whether Romanian bar authorities will take separate action.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The court spelled out the check it expects: every AI-generated reference verified against its own case-law database.

Risks and limitations

  • The applicants in the case lost one of their two representatives.
  • The court says verifying the faulty filing took considerable time away from its real functions.
