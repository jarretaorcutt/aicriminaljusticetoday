Cedar Rapids approves a nearly half-million-dollar Axon amendment to replace its 70 Flock plate readers

The city says the switch adds no cameras and saves nearly $127,000 a year; one council member voted no, calling for stronger privacy oversight, KCRG reports.

The Cedar Rapids City Council on Tuesday approved a nearly half-million-dollar contract amendment with Axon for new police surveillance technology, replacing the city's Flock license plate readers, KCRG reported on Nov. 17, 2026. Cedar Rapids has 70 automated license plate reader cameras.

The city says the switch will not add more cameras and will save nearly $127,000 a year. Police said they already use Axon for dash cameras and body cameras, that Axon will better connect license plate readers with that equipment, and that they are exploring outside audits of the technology.

The vote was not unanimous. Councilmember David Maier voted no, saying he was not opposed to the technology but was opposed to additional surveillance at this time. Councilmember Dale Todd voted yes but said more oversight could help address public distrust. Residents who spoke raised concerns about spending and drivers' privacy.

The city's contract with Flock expires in February, and police plan to phase in the Axon technology after that, KCRG reports.
