Justice Department ethics office finds two of its attorneys broke conduct rules in using AI and refers both for review

One assistant U.S. attorney filed a pleading with fabricated quotations and then misled the court about it. Another attorney uploaded documents covered by a protective order to a public AI platform.

The U.S. Justice Department's Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR), its internal ethics office, has published two investigative summaries about department attorneys' use of artificial intelligence. Both pages are marked updated November 17, 2026, and neither names the attorney.

In the first, OPR says an Assistant U.S. Attorney filed a pleading that misrepresented case holdings and included fabricated quotations. After the court ordered the government to address the allegations, the attorney filed a brief that did not say the errors came from his use of an AI platform and instead represented that they were due to inadvertently filing a draft document. At a hearing, OPR says, he made false and misleading representations to the court about his use of generative AI. OPR concluded he knowingly and intentionally violated his obligations under the rules of professional conduct and his duty of candor.

In the second, OPR says an attorney made unapproved use of a public artificial intelligence (AI) platform for government work. He was detected uploading a document with a header showing it was covered by a tribunal's protective order. Later reviews found more uploads, including a second document covered by the same order and documents containing Department employees' personally identifiable information, including medical information. OPR concluded he recklessly violated the rules of professional conduct by using the platform in violation of Department policy, and knowingly violated the rules and the protective order when he uploaded two documents he knew were covered.

OPR referred its findings in both matters to the Professional Misconduct Review Unit. The ABA Journal, citing Bloomberg Law, reported on November 19, 2026 that the unit can then make recommendations to state bar authorities.

What we know

  • OPR concluded an Assistant U.S. Attorney knowingly and intentionally violated professional conduct rules after a pleading with fabricated quotations (DOJ OPR, 2026 Investigative Summary 5, updated November 17, 2026).
  • OPR concluded a second attorney uploaded two documents covered by a protective order, and documents with employees' medical information, to a public AI platform (DOJ OPR, 2026 Investigative Summary 6).
  • Both sets of findings were referred to the Professional Misconduct Review Unit (DOJ OPR).
  • The unit can make recommendations to state bar authorities; neither lawyer is named (ABA Journal, November 19, 2026, citing Reuters and Bloomberg Law).

What remains unknown

  • Whether either attorney will face state bar discipline.
  • Which offices, courts and cases were involved; the summaries are anonymized.
  • Which AI platforms were used.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Publishing the findings shows the department applies its own AI and candor rules to its attorneys.

Risks and limitations

  • Anonymized summaries do not tell affected parties whether their case or their data was involved.
  • Documents under a court's protective order and employees' medical information were put on a public AI platform, according to OPR.
