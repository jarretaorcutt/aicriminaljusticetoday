Idaho Supreme Court releases a report on when and how state courts may use AI; it adopts no tool

A workgroup of judges, lawyers and court staff proposes a standing committee to advise the court on AI.

The Idaho Supreme Court has released a report on how the state's courts should decide whether to use artificial intelligence, the court announced in a notice dated 2026-10-19. The "AI Governance Implementation" report summarizes a year of work by judges, attorneys, IT professionals and court administrators, with input from the National Center for State Courts.

"Nothing in the report obligates or adopts an AI tool for court use," the court said. The workgroup developed guiding principles that put ethics, transparency and accountability first and require human oversight of any tool; the court says those principles were formally adopted earlier this year. The workgroup also proposes an ongoing court committee to advise the Supreme Court on AI, which is expected to advise on the Idaho State Bar's rules for attorneys as well.

The workgroup was convened by former Chief Justice G. Richard Bevan, who said: "our courts must be courts of principles, not of algorithms." Next steps rest with the Supreme Court. We relied on the court's announcement and have not reviewed the report itself.

What we know

  • The report was released with a court notice dated Oct. 19, 2026 (Idaho Supreme Court).
  • It adopts no AI tool and focuses on how to judge appropriate use (Idaho Supreme Court).
  • The workgroup proposes a standing committee to advise the court on AI (Idaho Supreme Court).

What remains unknown

  • Which specific uses, if any, the court will approve.
  • Whether the guidance addresses criminal sentencing or pretrial decisions; we have not reviewed the report.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Setting principles before buying tools gives judges a standard to test vendors against.

Risks and limitations

  • Principles without specific rules can leave individual courts to decide differently.
