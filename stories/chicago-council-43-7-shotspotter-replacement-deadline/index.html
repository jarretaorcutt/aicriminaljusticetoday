Chicago's City Council votes 43-7 to press for a gunshot detection contract within 30 days; the mayor says he won't rush

The measure follows a non-binding referendum backed by more than 72% of voters; Mayor Brandon Johnson says the city will test technologies in a pilot first.

Chicago's City Council voted 43-7 on Wednesday, Nov. 18, for a measure calling on the city's chief procurement officer to finalize negotiations and issue a new contract for acoustic gunshot detection technology within 30 days, CBS Chicago reported. If no contract is awarded within 60 days, the procurement officer would have to give a written explanation to the chair of the Committee on Contracting Oversight and Equity, which CBS Chicago said makes the vote largely symbolic.

The vote came two weeks after more than 72% of voters backed a non-binding referendum asking whether the city should restart gunshot detection technology. Mayor Brandon Johnson turned off ShotSpotter in 2024 and launched the process to find a replacement in February 2025, but no contract has been awarded.

Ald. Anthony Beale (9th) urged the mayor to turn ShotSpotter on immediately. Ald. David Moore (17th), who supported keeping ShotSpotter in 2024, voted no, saying forcing a vendor choice in 30 days could open the city to lawsuits.

Johnson said in a statement: "We will not repeat past procurement mistakes by rushing into costly, long-term contracts without evidence that the technology works." He said an ongoing request for proposals and a no-cost pilot will let the city evaluate the technologies' accuracy, reliability and protection of privacy and civil rights.

What we know

  • The council vote was 43-7 (CBS Chicago).
  • The measure sets a 30-day target and requires a written explanation if no contract is awarded within 60 days (CBS Chicago).
  • More than 72% of voters backed a non-binding referendum on restarting gunshot detection (CBS Chicago).
  • Mayor Brandon Johnson says he will complete a pilot before choosing a replacement (CBS Chicago).

What remains unknown

  • Which vendors will take part in the city's pilot and when it starts.
  • Whether a contract will be awarded within the council's 30-day target.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Supporters say gunshot alerts can bring police and paramedics to shootings that nobody reports to 911.

Risks and limitations

  • The mayor says the technology has not been proven effective and must protect privacy and civil rights (CBS Chicago).
