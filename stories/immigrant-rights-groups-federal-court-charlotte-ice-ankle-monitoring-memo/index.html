Immigrant rights groups ask a federal judge in Charlotte to make ICE publish its full memo expanding GPS ankle monitoring

The government says a copy that surfaced in a Pennsylvania court case makes the lawsuit moot. The judge asked both sides for written arguments.

A federal judge in Charlotte, North Carolina, heard arguments on Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026 over whether U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) must publish the agency's full memo expanding electronic monitoring of immigrants, WFAE reported.

According to WFAE, Carolina Migrant Network and the Amica Center for Immigrant Rights requested the memo through the Freedom of Information Act in October 2025 and sued in January after ICE did not respond. ICE later published a version with portions blacked out, which the groups argue hides information that should be public.

The June 2025 memo involves immigrants enrolled in ICE's Alternatives to Detention program, which monitors people living outside detention centers while their immigration cases proceed. WFAE reports that the memo directs officers to increase required check-ins and use GPS ankle monitors on immigrants, even on pregnant women.

Government attorneys said ICE mistakenly released the memo without redacting it, and argued that a copy made public in a Pennsylvania court case makes the lawsuit moot, the station reported. Carolina Migrant Network says it still has not officially received the full memo. Amica Center attorney Evan Benz said after the hearing that the groups want the document published, unredacted, on ICE's website.

The judge gave the nonprofits 10 days to explain in writing why they believe ICE should publish the full memo, and the government will then have 10 days to respond, according to WFAE.

The report describes GPS ankle monitors and check-ins; it does not describe any artificial intelligence in the program. We include it because electronic monitoring is the main automated tool of supervision outside detention, and the rules for using it are what the groups are asking to see.

What we know

  • Carolina Migrant Network and the Amica Center for Immigrant Rights requested the memo under the Freedom of Information Act in October 2025 and sued in January (WFAE).
  • The June 2025 memo directs officers to increase required check-ins and use GPS ankle monitors on immigrants in the Alternatives to Detention program, even on pregnant women (WFAE).
  • The judge gave the groups 10 days to file written arguments and the government 10 days to respond (WFAE).

What remains unknown

  • Whether the judge will order ICE to publish the unredacted memo.
  • What the blacked-out portions of the published version say.
  • How many people have been placed on GPS ankle monitors under the memo; the WFAE report gives no figure.

Risks and limitations

  • The groups argue that the redactions hide rules the public should be able to see about who is put on a GPS monitor (WFAE).
