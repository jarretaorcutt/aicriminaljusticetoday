Shawnee, Kansas, approves a $4.9 million Axon expansion with six police drones, plate readers and AI non-emergency call triage

The council voted 7-1 on Nov. 9; the contract extension runs from Feb. 1, 2027, to Dec. 31, 2034.

The Johnson County Post reports that the Shawnee City Council voted 7-1 at its Nov. 9 meeting to approve a 95-month expansion of the police department's Axon contract, with Councilmember Laurel Burchfield the lone dissenter. The existing agreement pays Axon $423,000 per year; the new contract adds about $198,000 per year and extends it to 2034. The extension runs from Feb. 1, 2027, to Dec. 31, 2034.

The Post reports the package includes six police drones, 10 portable license plate readers, a Fusus real-time operations center, and Prepared 911 & Automated Non-Emergency Triage (ANET), an AI-driven system in the dispatch center meant to process and divert non-emergency inquiries. Chief of Police Sam Larson told the council that if dispatch is too busy to pick up a non-emergency call, the AI could talk to the caller and ask questions before a dispatcher picks it up. License plate reader data is stored for 180 days, the Post reports.

What we know

  • The council voted 7-1 on Nov. 9 for the 95-month contract expansion; Councilmember Laurel Burchfield voted no (Johnson County Post).
  • The deal adds about $198,000 per year to an existing $423,000-per-year Axon agreement and extends it to 2034 (Johnson County Post).
  • It includes six police drones, 10 portable license plate readers and Prepared 911 & Automated Non-Emergency Triage (ANET) (Johnson County Post).
  • License plate reader data is stored for 180 days (Johnson County Post).

What remains unknown

  • How the AI triage system will be evaluated for errors with callers.
  • Whether the council will receive regular reports on drone and plate-reader use.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Police say the AI triage could keep non-emergency calls from tying up dispatchers during busy periods (Johnson County Post).

Risks and limitations

  • An AI system speaking with callers could misclassify a call that needs a human response.
