In Duplin County, North Carolina, one town awaits Flock cameras while the sheriff's office decides against them

From the archive (Oct 22, 2026): Wallace's police chief defended the readers; the sheriff's office cited the controversy.

At the Oct. 8 Wallace town council meeting, Police Chief Jimmy Crayton defended the Flock license plate cameras the town has approved, saying it will be several more months before they arrive, The Duplin Times reported on Oct. 22, 2026. The Duplin County sheriff's office had considered Flock cameras but, due to the controversy, decided against it, the paper reports. The Warsaw Police Department has had the cameras for several years. The article notes misuse cases elsewhere in the state, including a sheriff's deputy in Brunswick County who was indicted on charges of misusing the readers.

What we know

  • Wallace has approved Flock cameras that had not yet arrived (The Duplin Times).
  • The county sheriff's office decided against using Flock cameras (The Duplin Times).

What remains unknown

  • When Wallace's cameras will be installed and under what written policy.
