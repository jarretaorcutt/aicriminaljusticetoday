ICE is paying AT&T for access to raw phone data and analysts who search it, 404 Media reports

An internal ICE procurement document says the arrangement gives the agency "access to AT&T's raw data and experienced analysts." Experts call it a mass surveillance program.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement is paying AT&T millions of dollars to have AT&T's analysts sift through raw AT&T data for a wide variety of investigations, 404 Media reported on Nov 19, 2026, citing an internal ICE document, other procurement records and experts who reviewed them.

"This requirement provides ICE access to AT&T's raw data and experienced analysts," the document reads, according to 404 Media. The product, called Data Analytics and Support Services or Data Analytical Services, appears to be related to Hemisphere, an earlier program in which AT&T gave U.S. authorities at state, local and federal levels access to AT&T user data. That program shut down.

Civil liberty experts told 404 Media the data can include trillions of phone records and location data, and that non-customers are likely swept up because AT&T connects calls for other phone companies. AT&T told 404 Media the data is made available under a valid legal order; experts point out that can be an administrative subpoena, which agencies issue themselves without a judge's approval.

What we know

  • An internal ICE document says the requirement "provides ICE access to AT&T's raw data and experienced analysts" (404 Media).
  • The product appears related to Hemisphere, an earlier AT&T program that has shut down (404 Media).
  • AT&T says data is provided under a valid legal order; experts say that can be an administrative subpoena that needs no judge's approval (404 Media).

What remains unknown

  • The contract's exact value and term; 404 Media's free text says only "millions of dollars."
  • How many records ICE has searched and for which investigations.

Benefits and opportunities

  • AT&T says the data is made available under a valid legal order (404 Media).

Risks and limitations

  • Experts call it a mass surveillance program that can reach trillions of phone records and people who are not AT&T customers (404 Media).
