DHS has committed more than $2.9 billion to surveillance and data tools, Brennan Center finds

From the archive (Jul 29, 2026): Biometrics leads with more than $1 billion; commitments to Palantir rose from $20.4 million in 2024 to $162.2 million in 2026.

The Department of Homeland Security has committed more than $2.9 billion to surveillance, data collection and data analytics tools since the start of the Biden administration, the Brennan Center for Justice reported in a July 29 brief. Biometrics accounts for more than $1 billion spent or promised, including mobile biometric matching from NEC, which created the Mobile Fortify app; its privacy documentation says DHS will keep all photos for 15 years.

Commitments to Palantir rose from $20.4 million in 2024 to $162.2 million so far in 2026, the brief says, and DHS has taken down its policy on the use of facial recognition. The authors say the figures are minimums because federal contracting data is opaque.

What we know

  • DHS committed more than $2.9 billion to these tools since the start of the Biden administration (Brennan Center).
  • Biometrics accounts for more than $1 billion (Brennan Center).
  • Palantir commitments rose from $20.4 million in 2024 to $162.2 million in 2026 (Brennan Center).

What remains unknown

  • The full amount spent; the authors call the figures minimums.
