India's Supreme Court AI committee will consult industry experts before finalising rules that bar AI from assessing bail

Draft regulations released in June keep final authority with judicial officers and bar AI from assessing bail eligibility, witness credibility or flight risk.

The Supreme Court of India's AI committee has decided to hold a two-day meeting with industry experts before finalising its draft regulations on the use of artificial intelligence in the legal field, ThePrint reported on 19 November, 2026. The meeting is scheduled for December, and sources told ThePrint that committee members, including its chairperson Justice P.S. Narasimha, would attend.

The draft regulations, released in June, permit AI in judicial and legal work only in an assistive capacity, subject to strict human oversight. The ultimate authority to determine questions of law, fact and justice would rest exclusively with judicial officers, ThePrint reported. The proposed rules also bar the use of AI to assess bail eligibility, determine the credibility of parties or witnesses, or evaluate flight risk.

The draft would require lawyers to declare before arguments begin if they used AI to prepare their cases. ThePrint reported that the Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association has sought removal of that provision. The draft also prohibits AI-based surveillance or continuous monitoring of judicial officers, advocates, litigants and others within court premises.

Following the consultation, the committee will hold internal deliberations before notifying the regulations, a source told ThePrint.

What we know

  • The committee will hold a two-day meeting with industry experts in December (ThePrint).
  • The draft regulations were released in June (ThePrint).
  • The draft bars AI from assessing bail eligibility, the credibility of parties or witnesses, or flight risk (ThePrint).
  • The Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association has sought removal of the lawyer declaration rule (ThePrint).

What remains unknown

  • When the final rules will be notified.
  • Whether the lawyer declaration requirement will survive the consultation.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The draft keeps decisions on law, fact and justice with judicial officers (ThePrint).

Risks and limitations

  • Until the rules are notified, these safeguards do not yet bind Indian courts.
