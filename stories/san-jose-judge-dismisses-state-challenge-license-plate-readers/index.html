A Santa Clara County judge dismisses a state-constitution challenge to San Jose's network of more than 500 plate readers

From the archive (Oct 6, 2026): The ACLU and EFF had argued warrantless police searches of the data violate the California Constitution.

Santa Clara County Superior Court Judge Jeffrey El-Hajj dismissed a lawsuit challenging San Jose's network of license plate reader cameras, made up of more than 500 devices, San José Spotlight reported on October 6, 2026. The suit, brought by the ACLU and EFF for SIREN and the Council on American-Islamic Relations, argued that the city's warrantless police searches of the data violate the California Constitution; the judge rejected those arguments. The report notes the city had reduced how long it keeps the data from one year to 30 days. Plaintiffs said they were reviewing their legal options. A later appeal is covered in a related story.

What we know
