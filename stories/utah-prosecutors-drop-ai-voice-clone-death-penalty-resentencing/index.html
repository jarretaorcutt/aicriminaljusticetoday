Utah prosecutors drop their plan to play an AI clone of a murdered woman's voice at a death penalty re-sentencing

Weber County told the judge on Nov. 20 that the AI company refused a license and no other voice-cloning provider would work with it, KSL reports. The judge had not ruled on whether the clone could be used.

Prosecutors seeking a third death sentence in the murder of Joyce Yost have dropped their plan to use artificial intelligence voice cloning at a 2027 sentencing hearing, KSL reported on Nov. 20, 2026.

According to KSL, Yost gave sworn testimony at a preliminary hearing on June 12, 1985, and disappeared before the trial she was due to testify in. A text transcript of her words is preserved in court records, but no audio recording exists. In past hearings, prosecutors used human proxies to read her words from the transcript.

KSL reports that Weber County created a clone of Yost's voice by feeding snippets of a police interview recording into an AI tool from ElevenLabs, and in 2025 asked Utah 2nd District Court Judge Michael DiReda to approve using the clone to present her testimony at the re-sentencing. The defense resisted and, on August 10, 2026, brought two expert witnesses to court to argue the AI voice clone should not be allowed as evidence. After that hearing, ElevenLabs told Weber County it would not grant prosecutors a license to use its software, KSL reports.

Judge DiReda had not yet issued a final ruling on the request when Weber County Chief Criminal Deputy Attorney Branden Miles told him the county had not been able to find another AI voice-cloning provider willing to work with it, according to KSL. The re-sentencing is currently scheduled for September 2027. The Utah Supreme Court overturned the second death sentence in the case in 2024 and ordered a new sentencing hearing, KSL reports.

What we know

  • Weber County prosecutors are abandoning their effort to have a computer-generated simulation of Joyce Yost's voice played for the jury (KSL, Nov. 20, 2026).
  • The clone was made by feeding snippets of a police interview recording into an AI tool from ElevenLabs; the company later told the county it would not grant a license (KSL).
  • The defense brought two expert witnesses to an August 10, 2026 hearing to argue against the clone; Judge Michael DiReda had not issued a final ruling (KSL).
  • The re-sentencing is scheduled for September 2027 (KSL).

What remains unknown

  • How the judge would have ruled on admitting an AI voice clone; the request was dropped before a final ruling.
  • Why ElevenLabs refused the license; KSL's report does not give the company's reasons.
  • How her 1985 testimony will be presented in 2027; human proxies read it at past hearings.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The dispute put expert testimony about AI voice cloning on the record in a capital case before any jury heard it.

Risks and limitations

  • With no ruling, Utah courts still have no decision on whether a cloned voice can present a dead witness's testimony.
  • The outcome turned on vendors' choices, not on a legal standard other courts can apply.
