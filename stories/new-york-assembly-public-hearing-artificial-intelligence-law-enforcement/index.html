New York Assembly committees call a Nov. 30 public hearing on how law enforcement uses artificial intelligence

The Codes and Science & Technology committees want testimony on the reliability and accuracy of AI tools used by state and local law enforcement, and on the safeguards around them.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 23, 2026

News
Jurisdiction

AI application

The New York State Assembly's Standing Committee on Codes and its Standing Committee on Science & Technology will hold a joint public hearing titled "Artificial Intelligence in Law Enforcement" on November 30, 2026, at 250 Broadway in New York City, according to a notice the Assembly posted on Nov. 20, 2026. The purpose is to examine how AI is being used by state and local law enforcement.

The notice says the New York State Police uses AI through the New York State Intelligence Center, and that the New York City Police Department's Domain Awareness System uses AI-powered facial recognition, gunshot detection systems, social media monitoring tools and predictive policing software. It also lists concerns that the technology erodes privacy, reinforces racially biased policing practices and provides opportunities for abuse.

The committees, chaired by Assembly Members Jeffrey Dinowitz (Codes) and Steven Otis (Science & Technology), are seeking feedback on the reliability and accuracy of such tools, how information is collected and shared, and what safeguards are in place. Oral testimony is by invitation only and limited to 5 minutes.

The notice does not name specific bills, and a hearing does not by itself change any law.

What we know

  • The joint hearing is set for November 30, 2026 (NY State Assembly notice).
  • The notice cites AI use at the New York State Intelligence Center and the NYPD's Domain Awareness System (NY State Assembly notice).
  • The committees seek feedback on reliability and accuracy, data collection and sharing, and safeguards (NY State Assembly notice).
  • Oral testimony is by invitation only (NY State Assembly notice).

What remains unknown

  • Who has been invited to testify.
  • Whether the hearing leads to legislation.

Benefits and opportunities

  • Puts police AI tools on the public record before lawmakers.

Risks and limitations

  • Invitation-only testimony may leave out people directly affected by these tools.
