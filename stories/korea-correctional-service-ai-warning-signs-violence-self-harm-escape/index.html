South Korea's prison chief says AI will look for warning signs of violence, self-harm and escape, with treatment and parole left to staff

From the archive (Aug 27, 2026): Korea Correctional Service Commissioner General Lee Hong-yeon described the plan in a written interview with The Korea Times.

Korean prisons will use AI to identify warning signs of violence, self-harm and escape, Korea Correctional Service Commissioner General Lee Hong-yeon told The Korea Times in a written interview published Aug 27, 2026. He said AI could analyze counseling and behavioral-observation records, disciplinary actions and medical information together to detect early signs of risk, as part of the government's 2026-30 correctional policy agenda. Lee said a prisoner's treatment or parole will not be determined solely on the basis of AI analysis, that accuracy and error rates will be compared across groups to check for bias, and that reliability testing is planned before deployment.

What we know

  • AI would analyze counseling, disciplinary and medical records to detect early signs of risk (The Korea Times).
  • Treatment or parole will not be determined solely by AI analysis, Lee said (The Korea Times).

What remains unknown

  • When the system will be deployed and what error threshold it must meet.
