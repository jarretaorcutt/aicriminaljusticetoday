GAO: ICE can't tell whether officers turn on body cameras; DHS rejects automatic activation

From the archive (Oct 15, 2026): DHS agreed with six of seven GAO recommendations. ICE had issued no reprimands for camera-policy violations.

A Government Accountability Office audit found that ICE cannot reliably determine whether its personnel activate body cameras when required, Biometric Update reported on Oct. 15. DHS agreed with six of GAO's seven recommendations but rejected the one calling for additional controls such as automated activation.

GAO found CBP had issued one written reprimand for camera-policy noncompliance through September 2025, and ICE had issued none, according to the report. Both agencies use Axon equipment, and CBP already uses automatic activation in certain situations. DHS said a camera handbook is projected for June 30, 2027.

What we know

  • ICE cannot reliably determine whether officers activate cameras when required (GAO via Biometric Update).
  • DHS rejected one of GAO's seven recommendations, on automated activation (Biometric Update).
  • Through September 2025, CBP issued one reprimand for camera noncompliance and ICE none (Biometric Update).

What remains unknown

  • Whether required recordings were made at all; the compliance figures don't show it.
