ACLU and EFF appeal the dismissal of their challenge to San Jose's plate-reader network

San Jose Spotlight reports the groups are asking a higher court to revisit a ruling that drivers have no reasonable expectation of privacy on public streets.

San Jose Spotlight reported on November 25, 2026 that the American Civil Liberties Union and the Electronic Frontier Foundation have appealed a Santa Clara County Superior Court decision dismissing their lawsuit over San Jose's license plate reader network. The outlet describes a network of more than 500 cameras operated under a contract with Flock Safety. Judge Jeffrey El-Hajj dismissed the case, citing earlier court opinions finding that drivers have no reasonable expectation of privacy on public streets. Critics of the program say police can search the database without a warrant. Andrew Crocker of the EFF is quoted criticizing the ruling, and San Jose City Attorney Susana Alcala Wood is quoted defending the city's program. This story updates our earlier report on the dismissal. We have not reviewed the appeal filing itself.

What we know

  • The ACLU and EFF appealed the Superior Court dismissal (San Jose Spotlight).
  • The network has more than 500 cameras run under a contract with Flock Safety (San Jose Spotlight).
  • Judge Jeffrey El-Hajj cited earlier opinions on drivers' privacy on public streets when dismissing the case (San Jose Spotlight).

What remains unknown
