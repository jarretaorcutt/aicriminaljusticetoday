UK justice ministry pilots a tool that gives probation staff faster access to GPS tag data in three regions of England

The ministry says staff have run over 30,000 searches and that it aims to reach all probation regions by the end of 2026. The figures are its own.

By the AI in Criminal Justice Today newsroom · Published November 21, 2026
Story type
News
Jurisdiction
United Kingdom, England

The UK Ministry of Justice is piloting a tool that gives probation staff faster access to location data from electronic monitoring tags, according to a post on the ministry's Justice Digital blog dated 4 November 2026.

The post says the Electronic Monitoring Data Insights (EMDI) tool brings together information from across electronic monitoring services into a single platform, helping practitioners understand movement patterns, assess compliance and take action where necessary. It describes the tool as helping staff obtain and use GPS tagging information more efficiently, in place of time-consuming manual processes.

According to the ministry, EMDI is being piloted across the London, West Midlands and East of England probation regions, with thousands of users already carrying out over 30,000 searches. It says testing indicates the tool saves an average of 21 minutes per week for every person on probation whose trail monitoring data is reviewed. The post does not explain how that figure was measured.

The post quotes unnamed practitioners. One said: "Thanks to the data, I was able to recall him (the person on probation) due to non-compliance." Another said staff can "make professional judgment decisions in a significantly more informed way."

The ministry says its ambition is to roll the tool out across all probation regions by the end of 2026.

The post does not say that EMDI uses artificial intelligence, and it does not describe automated alerts or scoring. It gives no information about error rates in the location data or about how people on probation can challenge a decision based on it.

What we know

  • EMDI is being piloted across the London, West Midlands and East of England probation regions (Ministry of Justice, Justice Digital blog, 4 November 2026).
  • The ministry says thousands of users have carried out over 30,000 searches (Justice Digital blog).
  • The ministry says testing indicates a saving of 21 minutes per week for every person on probation whose trail monitoring data is reviewed (Justice Digital blog).
  • The ministry's ambition is to roll the tool out across all probation regions by the end of 2026 (Justice Digital blog).

What remains unknown

  • How the 21 minutes saving was measured.
  • Whether the tool uses any automated flagging or AI; the post does not say.
  • How often recalls or other enforcement decisions have relied on the tool, and how location errors are handled.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The ministry says quicker access to GPS data cuts administration and lets practitioners spend more time managing risk and working with people on probation (Justice Digital blog).

Risks and limitations

  • Easier access to movement data can lead to more recalls to prison; the post cites one recall and gives no data on accuracy or appeals.
