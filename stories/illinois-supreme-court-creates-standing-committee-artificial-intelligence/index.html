Illinois Supreme Court creates a standing Committee on Artificial Intelligence to set safeguards for AI in the courts

The committee of 18 members, chaired by retired Judge Jeffrey A. Goffinet, will study safe AI use and recommend training, oversight and governance.

The Illinois Supreme Court has announced the creation of the Supreme Court Committee on Artificial Intelligence, NewsChannel 20 (WICS/WRSP) reported on Nov. 24, 2026. The standing committee is charged with studying how artificial intelligence can be used safely and effectively within the judicial system.

According to the station, the committee's responsibilities will include evaluating risks related to data privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, and compliance with court records, and helping create safeguards and ethical standards for AI use in the courts. It will also examine how AI can streamline court processes while "preserving accuracy, fairness, and judicial independence," recommend training, oversight, and governance measures, and submit regular reports to the Supreme Court. The committee will have 18 members appointed by the Supreme Court, who can be reappointed for 3-year terms. The Hon. Jeffrey A. Goffinet (Ret.), a former co-chair of the court's AI Task Force, will chair it, and Madison County Judge Sara D. Smith will serve as vice chairperson. The Administrative Office of the Illinois Courts will provide administrative support.

What remains unknown

  • When the committee will issue its first report.
  • Whether it will propose new rules for lawyers' AI-assisted filings.

Benefits and opportunities

  • A standing body can keep court AI policy under review as the tools change.

Risks and limitations

  • Committees can lag behind how quickly lawyers, litigants and staff adopt AI tools.
