South Korea's 16.1 billion won trial-support AI sees minimal use, as judges report slow, error-prone searches

Edaily reports that none of this year's 4.239 billion won Phase 2 budget had been spent as of the end of last month.

Use of South Korea's court-built trial-support AI has remained minimal since it began full-scale operation in February, Edaily reported on Nov 23, 2026, even though the National Court Administration called this year the "first year of the judiciary's AI era."

The system is being built at a total cost of 16.1 billion won and is scheduled for completion by December 2028, according to Edaily. Judges who spoke to the outlet said its answers can contain errors or irrelevant lower-court precedents. One Seoul High Court judge said its response time is at least three times longer than that of commercial generative AI and that it lacks document summarization and issue analysis.

Edaily reports the court administration requested 19.078 billion won but received 16.1 billion won, about 84% of the request. Not a single won of the 4.239 billion won allocated for this year's Phase 2 project had been spent as of the end of last month, and a separate sentencing support AI project had a 0% execution rate for its 6.578 billion won budget. A judge on the judiciary's AI committee said only a single-digit number of GPUs are in operation.

The National Court Administration told Edaily it is aware of the feedback and will improve the system in stages, adding features through April of next year and support for drafting review reports by around May 2028.

What we know

  • The project costs 16.1 billion won and is scheduled for completion by December 2028 (Edaily).
  • None of the 4.239 billion won for this year's Phase 2 had been spent as of the end of last month (Edaily).
  • A separate sentencing support AI project has a 0% execution rate for its 6.578 billion won budget (Edaily).

What remains unknown

  • How many judges use the system and how often.
  • What the separate sentencing support AI will do and when it will be used.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The court administration says planned features will summarize first-instance litigation materials for judges (Edaily).

Risks and limitations

  • A slow or inaccurate research tool used by judges could surface wrong precedents, and a sentencing support AI is also in development.
