Fairfax County cuts plate-reader data retention to seven days and limits access to about 50 officers

The chairman's Board Matter, which the Board of Supervisors backed, also bars most data sharing with other agencies and directs a move away from Flock Safety.

The Fairfax County, Virginia, Board of Supervisors on November 17, 2026 backed new limits on the police department's automated license plate reader (ALPR) program, according to a statement from Chairman Jeffrey C. McKay's office. "The Board of Supervisors took strong action today," the statement says, and McKay thanked his colleagues for their support.

Under McKay's Board Matter, plate-reader data will be kept for seven days, instead of the previous standard set by Virginia law of no more than 21-days. Access is limited to officers assigned to the Real Time Crime Center, roughly 50 sworn officers, down from 296 users. Sharing with any other law enforcement agency is barred unless required by law until a regional data-sharing model is created through the Metropolitan Washington Council of Governments.

The Board Matter also keeps the September 15th, 2026 ban on new stationary readers, begins moving data from the county's Amazon Web Cloud servers to county-owned servers, directs staff to keep Flock from managing or accessing data from county cameras, and calls for a transition away from Flock Safety technology. The county will publish where the devices are and how locations are chosen.

McKay's statement says there have been no identified instances of misuse by the police department. The board scheduled a December 15th Safety and Security meeting for an update on what has been put in place.

What we know

  • Plate-reader data retention drops to seven days from the previous Virginia standard of no more than 21-days (Fairfax County Chairman's office).
  • Access is limited to roughly 50 sworn officers in the Real Time Crime Center, down from 296 users (Fairfax County Chairman's office).
  • The Board Matter calls for a transition away from Flock Safety technology and county-owned servers for the data (Fairfax County Chairman's office).
  • A December 15th Safety and Security meeting will review progress (Fairfax County Chairman's office).

What remains unknown

  • The board's vote count; the chairman's statement does not give one.
  • Which vendor will replace Flock Safety, and when.

Benefits and opportunities

  • The chairman says the technology has helped solve crimes and locate missing people (Fairfax County Chairman's office).

Risks and limitations

  • Residents raised concerns about privacy and data security, which the chairman says drove the changes (Fairfax County Chairman's office).
